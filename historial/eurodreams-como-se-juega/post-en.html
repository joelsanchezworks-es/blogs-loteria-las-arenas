EuroDreams: how to play, prizes and odds

Would you rather have a big jackpot all at once or an income every month? EuroDreams is the European game that pays its first prize as a monthly annuity for 30 years. We'll tell you how to play, what prizes it pays out and what the odds really are.

🎨

Banner · EuroDreams ticket with the grid of numbers from 1 to 40 and the Dream number

With EuroDreams you pick 6 numbers from 1 to 40 and 1 "Dream" number from 1 to 5, for €2.50 per bet, with draws on Monday and Thursday. Its first prize is not a one-off payment: it's an annuity of €20,000 a month for 30 years. The second prize, matching the 6 numbers, is €2,000 a month for 5 years.

It's a different kind of game from the ones with a rolling jackpot: here the star is the monthly income. Let's get into the rules, the prizes and the odds.

EuroDreams at a glance

How do you play EuroDreams?

 

Filling in a bet is simple. These are the steps:

1

Pick 6 numbers from 1 to 40

These are your main numbers. You can mark them yourself or let a random bet be generated for you.

2

Pick 1 Dream number from 1 to 5

This extra number is key: it's needed for the first prize. It goes separately, from 1 to 5.

3

Pay €2.50 per bet

That's the price of a single bet. You can play several bets or for several draws.

4

Wait for the draw: Monday or Thursday

EuroDreams is drawn twice a week, on Monday and Thursday. Keep your receipt.

It's a European game: the same draw is shared with other countries, although you play and get paid under Spain's rules.

In total value, that first-prize annuity works out to around €7.2 million over the 30 years. But the appeal is in the monthly "salary", not the one-off payment.

What are the odds of winning?

 

Let's be clear: the odds of the first prize are low, as in any game of this kind. Matching the 6 numbers plus the Dream has odds of 1 in 19,191,900. The second prize (the 6 numbers, without the Dream) is 1 in 4,797,975.

The lower categories are easier to hit, but they pay out small prizes. No number or combination "comes up more": they all have the same probability in each draw. Play for the fun of it, not expecting to win.

A big jackpot all at once or a monthly annuity?

That's the big difference from games like Euromillones. With Euromillones you chase a huge jackpot paid in one go (and whose first prize is quite a bit harder to match). With EuroDreams, the top prize is a fixed income every month for years, with better odds for that first category.

Neither is "better": it depends on what appeals to you more, the lump sum all at once or the monthly "salary". If you want to see the other approach, you can play Euromillones online and compare. Either way, sensibly and +18.

Frequently asked questions about EuroDreams

 
How do you play EuroDreams?

You pick 6 numbers from 1 to 40 and 1 Dream number from 1 to 5. Each bet costs €2.50 and the draws are on Monday and Thursday.

What is the first prize in EuroDreams?

An annuity of €20,000 a month for 30 years, by matching the 6 numbers and the Dream number. In total it works out to around €7.2 million paid month by month.

And the second prize?

By matching the 6 numbers (without the Dream): €2,000 a month for 5 years, around €120,000 in total. The lower categories pay out smaller amounts that vary by draw.

What are the odds of winning the first prize?

1 in 19,191,900. The second prize has odds of 1 in 4,797,975. They're long odds: EuroDreams is a game of chance, with no tricks or "hot" numbers.

What days is EuroDreams drawn?

Twice a week: Monday and Thursday. You can play for one draw or for several, and it's a good idea to keep your receipt safe.

How are the prizes taxed?

Lottery prizes in Spain are exempt up to a certain amount and the rest is subject to a special levy. The exempt amount and the rate can change, so it's worth checking the current tax rules. General information, not tax advice.
