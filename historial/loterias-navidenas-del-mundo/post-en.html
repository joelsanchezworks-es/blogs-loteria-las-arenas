Christmas lotteries around the world: curiosities you didn't know

Spain's Christmas Lottery feels unique to us, and in many ways it is. But we're not the only country that celebrates luck at the end of the year: Italy, Japan and the Netherlands have their own big draws. A stroll through the world's Christmas lotteries.

Spain's Christmas Lottery isn't the only date with luck at the end of the year: Italy, Japan and the Netherlands all have big Christmas draws, each with its own tradition. Some spread the money across many prizes, others bet on one big jackpot; some are drawn on New Year's Eve and others on Epiphany. Here are the most eye-catching curiosities.

In brief

Three draws, three traditions

Italy: the Lotteria Italia is drawn on Epiphany (6 January), like our Niño, and it was born tied to television. Japan: the year-end Jumbo is the biggest lottery in Asia. The Netherlands: the Staatsloterij is the oldest state lottery still running in the world, and its New Year's Eve draw is the most eagerly awaited. These are general facts; the exact prizes and dates change every year.

Italy: the Lotteria Italia, sister of our Niño

 

In Italy, the big draw arrives on Epiphany, 6 January, just like our Lotería del Niño. The Lotteria Italia has existed since 1956 and for decades has gone hand in hand with television: its draw airs within a popular programme on Rai, Italy's public broadcaster.

A curious fact: its record for tickets sold dates back to 1988, with more than 37 million. Unlike our Christmas draw, which spreads the money across a huge number of prizes, the Italian one concentrates its appeal on a few very big prizes.

Japan: the year-end Jumbo, Asia's giant

 

In Japan, the lottery is called Takarakuji, and its star draw is the year-end Jumbo (Nenmatsu Jumbo). It's held once a year, usually right on the cusp of the New Year, and it's considered the biggest lottery draw in all of Asia.

Like the Spanish one, it's part of the festive ritual: queuing up to buy at the "luckiest" sales points is a classic December sight in cities like Tokyo.

The Netherlands: the oldest lottery in the world

 

The Dutch Staatsloterij is considered the oldest state lottery still running in the world: its forerunner was created in 1726, with its first draw at the Ridderzaal in The Hague. It was born, like so many, to raise money for the State's coffers.

Its most eagerly awaited event is the New Year's Eve draw (the Oudejaarstrekking), which every year is crowned the most popular. While we open the festive season with Christmas, the Dutch close it with their big year-end draw.

And what's so special about the Spanish one?

Compared with the single huge jackpots of other countries, Spain's Christmas Lottery stands out for spreading the money across a huge number of prizes: El Gordo isn't a single jackpot but a shower of prizes all across the country. On top of that, we string together two big draws —the Christmas draw on 22 December and the Niño on 6 January—, something uncommon outside Spain. If your curiosity is piqued about its origins, you'll find it in the history of the Christmas Lottery.

Frequently asked questions about Christmas lotteries around the world

 
Which countries have a Christmas or year-end lottery?

Many. Among the best known: Spain (Christmas and Niño), Italy (Lotteria Italia, on Epiphany), Japan (the year-end Jumbo) and the Netherlands (with its New Year's Eve draw).

Which is the oldest lottery in the world?

The Staatsloterij of the Netherlands is considered the oldest state lottery still running: its forerunner dates back to 1726, with its first draw at the Ridderzaal in The Hague.

Is the Italian lottery like the Spanish one?

They're alike in the date: the Lotteria Italia is drawn on Epiphany (6 January), like our Niño. But it concentrates the prize money in a few big jackpots, while our Christmas draw spreads it across a huge number of prizes.

What's the year-end lottery in Japan like?

The year-end Jumbo (Nenmatsu Jumbo), part of the Takarakuji system, is the biggest draw in Asia. It's held once a year, close to the New Year, and draws crowds to the most popular sales points.

What makes Spain's Christmas Lottery special?

That it spreads the money across a huge number of prizes (El Gordo is a shower of prizes, not a single jackpot) and that we string together two big draws: the Christmas draw on 22 December and the Niño on 6 January.

Can I play other countries' lotteries?

It depends on each country's regulations. In Spain, the usual and safe option is to play the official draws through an authorised lottery outlet. The details of foreign draws change every year: it's worth confirming them with official sources.
