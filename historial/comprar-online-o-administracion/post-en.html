Buying the Christmas Lottery online or at the lottery office isn't a question of “good or bad”: both options are safe as long as they're official. What changes is the convenience, how you keep your ticket and how you check for a prize.

The real risk isn't choosing online or in person, but buying outside a certified official lottery office (unauthorised websites, resale with a mark-up…). We take that part as a given: however you buy, always do it at an official outlet.

That said, let's compare the two ways honestly so you can choose the one that suits you best.

In brief

Both are good if they're official; choose by convenience

Online and in person are equally safe at an official lottery office, and the ticket is worth exactly the same. Online you gain in convenience (you buy from home and can leave the ticket in safekeeping or request delivery); at the lottery office you gain in face-to-face service and having the ticket in your hand straight away. The key isn't the channel, but that it's official and certified.

Security: what really matters

 

Whether online or at the counter, an official lottery office gives you the same guarantees: an official ticket, the official price and a guaranteed prize payout. The difference in security isn't between online and in person, but between official and non-official.

The real risk is unauthorised websites and resale with a mark-up. So whichever channel you choose, the important thing is to check that you're at a certified official lottery office (below we give you a checklist to spot one in seconds).

Proof of purchase and safekeeping: don't let your prize slip away

 

This is where online brings extra peace of mind. If you buy online and leave the ticket in deposit and safekeeping, it's kept and checked by the lottery office: there's no risk of losing it, getting it wet or letting it expire in a drawer.

If you buy at the counter, you have the physical ticket straight away —something many people love— but the responsibility of keeping and checking it is yours. Sign the back, store it carefully and note down the draw date.

Delivery and convenience

 

Online you choose how you want it: leave it in safekeeping or receive it at home by delivery. You buy from the sofa, any time and with no queues, something that's especially welcome in the weeks before Christmas.

At the lottery office, on the other hand, you take the ticket with you on the spot and can sort out any questions with a real person. If you like face-to-face service or want the ticket in your hand right now, this is your option.

Frequently asked questions about buying the Christmas Lottery online or in person

 
Is it safe to buy the Christmas Lottery online?

Yes, as long as you do it on the official website of a certified lottery office of the National Lottery. In that case you have the same guarantees as at the counter: an official ticket, the official price and a guaranteed payout, with the added bonus of being able to leave it in safekeeping or request delivery. The risk isn't “online” in itself, but buying on unauthorised or resale websites.

What's the difference between buying online and at the lottery office?

Above all, convenience and how you keep your ticket. Online you buy from home at any time and can leave it in safekeeping (they keep and check it) or request delivery. At the lottery office you take the physical ticket with you straight away and get personal service. Both are equally safe if they're official, and the ticket is worth exactly the same.

Does the price change if I buy online?

No. At an official lottery office you always pay the official price of the ticket (20 €), online or at the counter, with no commissions or surcharges. If a website charges you more for the same ticket, that's a warning sign: be wary of mark-ups.

If I buy online, how do I know my ticket is safe?

With deposit and safekeeping, your ticket is kept and checked by the lottery office, and they let you know if there's a prize: it doesn't depend on a slip of paper you might lose. If you prefer home delivery, you receive it physically. Either way, always keep the proof of purchase the lottery office gives you.

How do I recognise a trustworthy lottery office?

In just a few seconds: check that it's an Official Lottery Office of the National Lottery (with its number on display), that the price is the official one (20 €), that the website —if you buy online— is the official one for that lottery office, that they give you a clear receipt and that it has physical details (address and phone). If any of these points is missing, better not to buy there.

Can I combine the two ways?

Of course. Many people buy a ticket or two at their usual lottery office and, on top of that, others online for convenience or for the syndicate. They're not mutually exclusive: the important thing is that, in both cases, you buy at an official outlet and keep your receipts.
