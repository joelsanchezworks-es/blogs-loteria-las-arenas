Filling in a Quiniela (Spain's state football pools) properly is simple: you mark a 1, an X or a 2 for the first fourteen matches and get the exact score right in the fifteenth, the famous Pleno al 15 (the bonus 15th-match bet). The problem usually isn't there but in the sums: miscalculating how many bets a Quiniela with doubles (two picks on one match) and triples (all three) generates is the error that ruins the most syndicates (peñas). It's not football; it's arithmetic.

Not because people get matches wrong — that's part of the game — but because nobody is clear on how much the bet costs, who has put in what, or how many columns are really in play. Human error, once again, counts for more than bad luck.

The quick answer

The costly mistake isn't getting a match wrong, it's miscalculating the columns

A Quiniela is 14 matches predicted as 1 (home win), X (draw) or 2 (away win), plus the Pleno al 15, where you have to get each team's exact number of goals right (0, 1, 2 or M, where M means 3 or more). Each bet costs €0.75. If you add doubles and triples, the number of bets multiplies: 2 raised to the number of doubles, times 3 raised to the number of triples. That calculation, not the football, is where syndicates come unstuck.

How to fill in a Quiniela, step by step

 

Filling in the coupon (boleto) takes little skill if you work through it in order. Here are the steps, from start to finish:

1

Mark the first 14 matches

For each one, pick 1 (the home team wins), X (draw) or 2 (the away team wins). Just one sign per match if you're playing a single bet.

2

Complete the Pleno al 15

In match number 15 it's not enough to get the winner right: you have to get the score right. Mark the home team's goals and the away team's goals separately: 0, 1, 2 or M (3 or more goals).

3

Choose how many bets you play

The minimum bet is 2 columns, that is, €1.50 (at €0.75 each). You can fill in several different columns or use doubles and triples on a single one.

4

Check, confirm and keep the receipt (resguardo)

Check that the signs are the ones you wanted, validate the coupon and keep the receipt. No receipt, no prize to collect.

The Pleno al 15: the box that confuses people most

 

The Pleno al 15 works differently from the other fourteen: instead of a 1, an X or a 2, you have to get right how many goals each team scores in the last match. The options for each team are 0, 1, 2 or M, where M means ‘three goals or more’. In other words, you make two predictions: the home team's goals and the away team's goals.

And here's the key point many people don't know: the Pleno al 15 only pays out if you have first got all fourteen previous matches right. It isn't a separate prize; it's the cherry on top that separates the Special category (14 correct results + Pleno) from the rest. If you get even one of the fourteen wrong, your Pleno al 15 doesn't count, however perfectly you nailed the score.

So when it comes to spreading your effort, it makes sense to focus first on getting the fourteen right and to treat the Pleno al 15 as what it is: the tie-breaker that, if it comes off, multiplies the prize. When a matchday ends with no top-category winners, that jackpot rolls over to the next one.

The prize categories, from highest to lowest, are: Special (14 correct results + Pleno al 15), First (14 correct results), and then Second, Third, Fourth and Fifth, which reward those who get 13, 12, 11 and 10 matches right. Overall, La Quiniela puts around 55% of its takings towards prizes. Put another way: you don't need to nail all fifteen to win something — with 10 correct results you're already in the money — but the big jackpot, the Pleno one, demands that you get everything right.

Doubles and triples: multiple betting without nasty surprises

 

A double means marking two signs on one match; a triple, all three. You cover more possible results, but each double multiplies the number of bets by two and each triple multiplies it by three. The formula is simple and worth keeping to hand: bets = 2 (raised to the number of doubles) × 3 (raised to the number of triples), and the cost is that number of bets times €0.75.

The maximum per coupon in the direct multiple method is 7 doubles and 5 triples (31,104 bets). There are also reduced bets (reducidas) and conditional bets (condicionadas), which cover a selection of combinations to bring the cost down while guaranteeing a certain number of correct results.

The most typical syndicate mistake is marking doubles and triples ‘by eye’ and getting a shock at validation: a few triples send the price soaring without anyone noticing. Four triples already come to €60.75; add a couple more doubles and you're into serious figures. Before you fill in the coupon, do the sum: multiply, look at the total and split it between the syndicate members. That way nobody overpays and nobody gets a surprise.

A worked example, syndicate in hand

Picture a syndicate of ten friends. You decide to play 3 doubles and 2 triples: that's 2³ × 3² = 8 × 9 = 72 bets, which at €0.75 comes to €54. Split ten ways, that's €5.40 a head. But if someone gets carried away and puts in 5 triples instead of 2, you jump to 3⁵ = 243 bets (€182.25): more than €18 per person. The same coupon, more than three times as expensive, just for marking a few extra boxes without doing the sum. That's why you work the figure out beforehand, rather than discovering it at the till.

Reduced bets: covering more for less

When you want to cover a lot of uncertain matches without the price shooting up, there are reduced and conditional bets. Instead of covering every possible combination, they cover a calculated selection that guarantees a minimum number of correct results if your base prediction is right. You pay considerably less in exchange for giving up some of the combinations. For a syndicate that wants to play several doubles and triples without going broke, they're usually the most efficient option: more coverage for every euro invested.

Do the sum before you validate, not after. The cost of a multiple Quiniela grows very fast: each triple multiplies by three. Work out the total (bets × €0.75) and split it between the participants before you pay. It's the simplest way to stop a syndicate ending up with someone putting in more than they thought.

The great syndicate mistake: heart versus statistics

 

The difference between a syndicate that adds up and one that throws money away isn't luck, but how it decides its predictions: with the heart or with the data. Always betting on your team to win, or putting triples on the matches you're passionate about rather than the genuinely uncertain ones, is the quickest way to waste the bet.

✓Save your doubles and triples for the most evenly matched games (the ones the bookmakers and the statistics see as uncertain), not for your favourites.

✓Lock in the clear matches with a single sign, so you don't spend multipliers where they aren't needed.

✓Decide as a group and by the data: form, league position, injuries, head-to-heads… not by loyalties.

✗Putting a 1 on your team no matter what: the heart doesn't get results right, and on top of that it skews the whole syndicate.

✗Loading the big games with triples: you spend the budget on excitement, not on probability.

At heart, La Quiniela rewards a cool head: it's not about guessing, but about spreading your doubles and triples well across the areas of greatest real uncertainty. The passionate fan fills in the coupon with the heart; the one who collects, with judgement. And when a syndicate decides, better still: several people debating the doubtful matches with the data in front of them get more right than one person running on faith in their team.

How to set up a Quiniela syndicate without the hassle

 

A Quiniela syndicate is a small partnership, and as such it needs clear accounts from minute one. These steps will help you avoid the usual arguments:

1

Work out the cost before you play

Decide the structure (doubles and triples), apply the formula and get the total. Only then will you know how much each person puts in.

2

Put in writing who takes part and with how much

A list with each member's name and stake, dated. If there's a big prize, it's what stops the payout being treated as a gift.

3

Keep the receipt (and a copy)

The validated receipt is the only claim to the prize. Take a photo and share it in the group so everyone has it.

4

Agree the split in advance

Make clear, before the match, what share each person gets. The accounts are settled with the coupon in hand, not with the prize.

It may seem over the top for a bet of a few euros, but the Quiniela syndicates that last for years are, without exception, the ones that write everything down. Paperwork doesn't take away the fun; it takes away the arguments. And the day a big prize lands — which is the whole point — that paperwork is what will turn the joy into a clean payout with no sour faces.

Frequently asked questions about La Quiniela

 
How do you fill in a football Quiniela step by step?

You mark a prediction for each of the 14 matches: 1 if you think the home team wins, X if you expect a draw and 2 if the away team wins. Then you complete the Pleno al 15, getting the number of goals for each team in the last match right (0, 1, 2 or M, which is 3 or more). The minimum bet is 2 columns, €1.50 (at €0.75 each). Finally, you validate the coupon and keep the receipt, which is the only document you can collect a prize with.

How does the Pleno al 15 work?

It's the fifteenth match on the coupon and it works differently: instead of 1, X or 2, you have to get the exact score right, marking the home team's and the away team's goals (0, 1, 2 or M = 3 or more goals). The key thing is that it only pays out if you have first got the 14 previous matches right: that's what sets the Special category (14 + Pleno) apart from the rest. If a matchday has no top-category winners, the jackpot rolls over to the next one.

How do you calculate La Quiniela's doubles and triples?

With a simple formula: bets = 2 (raised to the number of doubles) × 3 (raised to the number of triples), and the cost is that number times €0.75. For example, 3 doubles are 2³ = 8 bets (€6); 4 triples are 3⁴ = 81 bets (€60.75); and 2 doubles with 2 triples are 2²×3² = 36 bets (€27). The maximum per coupon in direct multiple betting is 7 doubles and 5 triples, that is, 31,104 bets. Always work out the total before you validate.

How much does it cost to play La Quiniela?

Each bet (column) costs €0.75, and the minimum bet is 2 columns: €1.50. From there, the price depends on how many bets you generate: a single is very cheap, but as soon as you add doubles and triples the cost multiplies quickly.
