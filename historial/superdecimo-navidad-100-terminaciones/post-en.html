What is the Superdécimo de Navidad?

 

It's a pack that brings together 100 décimos from the Christmas Draw, one for each possible ending: from 00 to 99. Instead of picking one number and crossing your fingers over its ending, you take one of each.

At the official price, each décimo costs €20, so the full pack adds up to €2,000 (100 × €20). The point isn't the up-front price, but what you secure by covering every ending, which is what we'll look at next.

Note: in our administration we call this pack of 100 endings the "Superdécimo". If you were looking for the superdécimo as an individual gift, that's a different product; check with us before buying.

The "trick": why each décimo works out at about €15

 

The Christmas Draw pays prizes not only on the full numbers, but also by ending and by refund (the last digit). If you hold a décimo for each ending, come what may you land those prizes tied to the Gordo's ending and to the refunds.

That's why people talk about around €500 secured: it isn't about guessing the Gordo —that's still chance—, but about covering all 100 endings so you don't miss out on the ending and refund prizes. That return lowers your outlay: out of €2,000, you get back on the order of €500, so each décimo works out at about €15 in practice.

The exact amount secured depends on the specific structure of each year's draw, so it's worth confirming with the administration when you reserve the pack. The €500 figure is a guide, not a guarantee of the outcome.

And all 100 décimos still play for the Gordo

 

The key point: securing that return doesn't take you out of the big draw. The pack's 100 décimos play for every prize —the Gordo, second, third, fourth and fifth prizes, and the pedrea— exactly like any other décimo.

That said, let's be honest: holding 100 different endings doesn't multiply your chances at the Gordo the way people sometimes think. The Gordo falls on one specific number, and you're playing 100 numbers; your odds of taking the top prize are those of playing 100 décimos, no more and no less. What the pack secures are the ending and refund prizes, not the Gordo.

Is it a safe investment?

No. It's the lottery, and the lottery isn't an investment. The pack lowers the effective cost and secures a part of it, but you're still putting up money that, barring a top prize, you won't get back in full. The big prizes have low odds.

See it for what it is: a way to play big at Christmas by covering every ending, with the excitement intact and your feet on the ground. Play responsibly and only with what you want to set aside for it.
