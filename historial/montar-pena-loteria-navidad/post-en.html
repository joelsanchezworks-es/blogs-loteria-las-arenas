Playing the Christmas Lottery as a pool — with family, friends or workmates — is one of the best traditions of December. Sharing a décimo makes the excitement even greater… as long as everything is clear from the start.

Most of the trouble in pools does not come from bad faith, but from improvising: nobody wrote down who put in how much, no one knows who is keeping the décimo, or the split was never put in writing. All of that is avoided with a few minutes of organisation.

Setting up a Christmas Lottery pool without any scares is simple if you follow these steps.

In brief

A clear pool can be set up in five minutes

Appoint an organiser, make a list of who is taking part and how much each one puts in, split the shares in writing (so they add up to 100 %), decide who keeps the décimo (and leave a record with a photo showing the number) and agree on what you will do if you win. With that, the pool is shielded against misunderstandings. This is general information, not legal or tax advice.

The steps to set up a pool without any fuss

 

Order matters: organise first, then play. These are the basic steps of any well-run pool:

1

Appoint an organiser

A single person coordinates: they collect the money, buy the décimo, keep it and communicate the split. They have no more say than anyone else; they simply centralise things.

2

Make the list of participants

Write down the name of each participant and how much they contribute. The sooner, the better: that way nobody is left out on a 'word-of-mouth' basis.

3

Split the shares in writing

Work out what part of the décimo corresponds to each person according to what they put in. The total must add up to 100 %.

4

Buy the décimo and keep it safe

The organiser buys the décimo at an official lottery office and keeps it in a safe place (or leaves it in safekeeping if it is bought online).

5

Leave a record

Share a photo of the décimo showing the number, along with the split list, with all the participants.

6

Agree what to do if you win

Make it clear in advance how the prize will be collected and split, so that on that day there is nothing but joy.

The organiser: the key piece

 

Every pool needs someone to centralise things, and it is best if it is a person who is organised and trusted by everyone. Their role is not to 'be the boss', but to take care of the practical side: gathering the contributions, buying the décimo, keeping it safe and keeping the group informed.

A good organiser does not improvise from memory: they rely on the written list. That way, if someone asks 'how much do I have in?', the answer is always there to see and no one has to trust someone else's recollection.

The list of participants and the split

 

The heart of a pool without any fuss is a clear list: who takes part and with how much. From that comes the split of the shares, which is simply the part of the prize that would go to each person. Here is an illustrative example based on a €20 décimo:

Who keeps the pool's décimo?

 

It is the question that causes the most tension, and the answer is simple: the organiser keeps it, but with a record for everyone. As soon as it is bought, take a photo of the décimo clearly showing the number and share it with the pool along with the split list. That way everyone knows which number they are playing and with what share.

If you prefer not to depend on a physical slip, you can buy the Christmas Lottery online and leave the décimo in safekeeping at the lottery office: it is stored and checked, with no risk of it being lost or damaged. Whichever option you choose, the essential thing is that it is clear who has it and where.

What to do if you win: the shared prize

 

If the pool wins, the prize is split among the participants according to their shares. The key is to have documented everything: with the split list, each person receives exactly the agreed part, with no arguments.

For tax purposes, lottery prizes have an exempt portion (the first €40,000) and are taxed at 20 % on the rest. In a shared prize, the usual practice is to identify all the co-owners when collecting, so that each one is charged what corresponds to their part. This is general information: for a specific case, check with the lottery office where you collect or with an adviser.

Frequently asked questions about setting up a Christmas Lottery pool

 
How do you organise a Christmas Lottery pool?

In five steps: appoint an organiser, make a list of who takes part and how much they contribute, split the shares in writing (so they add up to 100 %), buy and keep the décimo while leaving a record (a photo showing the number) and agree in advance what will be done if you win. Setting it up takes a few minutes and avoids almost all misunderstandings.

Who should keep the pool's décimo?

The usual practice is for the organiser to keep it, but with transparency: as soon as it is bought, it is a good idea to share a photo of the décimo with the number visible and the split list with all the participants. Another option is to buy it online and leave it in safekeeping at the lottery office, so that it is stored and checked without depending on a slip of paper.

How are the shares split?

In proportion to what each participant contributes. If someone puts in twice as much as another, they are entitled to twice the share. What matters is that the contributions add up to the price of the décimo and that the shares add up to 100 %, and that everything is in writing and shared with the group.

What happens if a pool wins?

The prize is split among the participants according to their shares. Lottery prizes have an exempt portion (€40,000) and are taxed at 20 % on the rest. In a shared prize, the usual practice is to identify all the co-owners when collecting so that each one is charged their part. This is general information; for your case, check at the lottery office or with an adviser.

Do you need a contract to set up a pool?

It is not compulsory, but it is highly recommended to put in writing who takes part and with how much. Nothing complicated is needed: a clear list, signed or shared with everyone, is enough to avoid misunderstandings and to make it easier to collect if the décimo turns out to be a winner.
