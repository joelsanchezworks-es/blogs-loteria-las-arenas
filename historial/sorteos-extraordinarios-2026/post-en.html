What is a Lotería Nacional special draw?

 

A special draw is a special edition of the Lotería Nacional that pays out many more prizes than an ordinary Thursday or Saturday draw. To achieve that, it issues more series of each number, the décimo costs more (€12–20, as against the €6 of a Saturday) and the prize fund is far larger, with more generous prize tiers and aproximaciones all round.

The real star of these draws is the special prize: a very large sum that, unlike the first prize, falls on a single décimo — on one fraction and one series alone. It's what makes it possible for a single ticket in a special draw to deliver a tidy sum that would be completely unthinkable on a normal Saturday. The Christmas one is the most famous of all, but it's far from the only one: each and every special draw has its own.

What's more, each special draw has a personality all of its own. Some are tied to a much-loved date — Epiphany with El Niño, Father's Day, Hispanidad — while others, like the Red Cross draw, put part of the proceeds towards a good cause, so that playing is also a way of doing your bit. That mix of bigger prizes and good reasons to take part is what turns the special draws into the most eagerly awaited dates in the lottery year, and it's also why their décimos sell out far sooner than those of any ordinary Saturday. Getting to know them and knowing when they fall is, therefore, more useful than it might seem.

How it differs from a normal Saturday

 

The difference between a special draw and an ordinary Saturday comes down, above all else, to the sheer amount of prize money that gets handed out. An ordinary Saturday pays a first prize of €60,000 per décimo; a special draw pays a special prize that can run well into the millions on a single décimo, along with a great many more prizes shared out besides. Here are the key points:

→ A pricier décimo: €12–20, as against the €6 of an ordinary Saturday. You pay a bit more because the prize fund is a great deal bigger.

→ A special prize on one décimo: this is the big difference. A very large sum that falls on a single fraction and series, rather than being shared out among many.

→ More series and more prizes: more décimos are issued and far more money is shared out, with more generous aproximaciones and prize tiers throughout.

→ Notable dates: because they're played on particular days (Epiphany, the summer holidays, Hispanidad…), they carry a special excitement all of their own and their numbers sell out that much sooner.

In short, a special draw is really an ordinary Saturday 'levelled up'. The décimo costs a little more, but in return you're taking part in a draw that pays out a great deal more. Hence the advice: keep them all noted down and buy your tickets in good time.

Frequently asked questions

 
Which Lotería Nacional special draws are there in 2026?

Across 2026 there are several special and extraordinary draws, among them: El Niño (6 January), the Valentine's Day special draw (14 February), the Father's Day draw (21 March), the Red Cross draw (6 June), the Holiday draw (4 July), Hispanidad (12 October), the Constitution draw (5 December) and the Spanish Christmas Lottery (22 December), as well as El Niño 2027 (6 January). There are a few more specials dotted across the year besides (the Mother's Day draw, AECC…). Always confirm the exact date through the official channel before you play.

How does a special draw differ from a normal Saturday?

In that it pays out a great many more prizes. To manage that, the special draw issues more series, the décimo costs more (between €12 and €20, as against the €6 of an ordinary Saturday) and the prize fund is far larger. Its great hallmark is the special prize: a very large sum that falls on a single décimo — one fraction and one series — instead of being shared out. In every other respect the mechanics are identical: you buy your décimo and you take part in all the prizes in that draw.

How much does a special-draw décimo cost?

It all depends on the draw, but in 2026 a special-draw décimo costs between €12 and €20, compared with the €6 of an ordinary Saturday. The Red Cross draw, for example, costs €15, while El Niño and Christmas are €20. You pay more because the prize share-out is far bigger: in exchange for that décimo you're entering a draw that can pay out hundreds of millions, with a special prize of several million on a single ticket.

Is there a number to win the lottery today in 2026?

No — neither in the special draws nor in any other. In the Lotería Nacional the décimos already come with their number printed on them, and they all have exactly the same probability of winning: there are no 'lucky' numbers and no magic dates that improve your chances, however special the particular draw may be. Choose the décimo that appeals to you — a date, or an ending you happen to like — and play responsibly. Anyone trying to sell you 'today's winning number' is simply deceiving you.

How does the 1220 code work for winning?

It doesn't. The '1220 code' (or '1220 method') is numerology that does the rounds on social media, promising to predict the next number from the last result. It's of no use whatsoever, and no more so in the special draws: each draw is independent and random, so no calculation based on past results can possibly guess the next one. In the Lotería Nacional, what's more, the décimos are pre-printed — so there's simply nothing to 'calculate'. It's a hook to pull in clicks, not a genuine method.
