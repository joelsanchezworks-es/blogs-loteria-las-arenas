Every December, half of Spain asks itself the same question: where has the Christmas Gordo landed? And the answer tends to surprise, because the Gordo (the top prize) almost never falls in a single town: it is shared out across several provinces at once.

The reason isn't the luck of any particular place, but how the Christmas Lottery is sold. Understanding it helps you read the headlines about 'where the Gordo has landed' correctly and, along the way, avoid falling for myths about 'lucky' lottery offices.

Let's look at it clearly: how the Gordo is shared out across the map, whether some places get more prizes, and where to look up the official data.

In brief

The Gordo doesn't fall in one place: it is shared out

The same Christmas Lottery number is sold at many lottery offices all over Spain, so when the Gordo comes up it is usually shared out among several provinces and towns. There are no 'luckier' places: a lottery office shares out more prizes when it sells more tickets (décimos), not because chance prefers it. Every number has exactly the same probability, wherever you buy it.

Why the Gordo never falls in a single place

 

The key lies in how the Christmas Lottery is issued. Each number (from 00000 to 99999) isn't printed just once: it is issued in many identical series. And each full ticket is split into ten décimos of €20 each.

That means the same number —including the one that ends up being the Gordo— is spread out as thousands of décimos across lottery offices all over the country. That's why, when it wins, the prize appears at once in very different cities and towns: it isn't that it landed 'in one place', it's that it was sown all over the map.

That's why the headlines about 'where the Gordo has landed' mention several places: the prize is shared out wherever the décimos of that number were sold.

Are there 'luckier' provinces or towns?

 

It's the great myth of the Gordo, and it's worth saying honestly: no, there are no places luckier than others. If a lottery office or a province often appears in the news, it's almost always because it sells a lot of décimos: the greater the volume, the greater the chances of sharing out some prize. It's statistics, not magic.

The same goes for the famous lottery offices: they share out a lot because they sell a lot, not because they 'bring luck'. Chance has no memory and no map: every number in the draw has the same probability of being the Gordo, whether it was sold in a large capital or a small town.

Where to check where the Gordo has landed (official data)

 

If you want to know exactly where the Gordo landed in a particular year, the only reliable source is the official one. The Sociedad Estatal Loterías y Apuestas del Estado (SELAE) publishes, after each draw, the list of prizes and the places where they were sold.

There you'll see that the Gordo —and the second and third prizes too— appears shared out across different provinces and towns. Be wary of rankings or 'luck statistics' that promise to predict where it will land: the past doesn't foretell the future, because every draw starts from scratch.
