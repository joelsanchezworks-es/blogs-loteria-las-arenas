What is a Christmas lottery billete?

 

A billete is the complete set of the 10 décimos of one number (from the same series). That's where the word comes from: in the past, the whole billete was sold as one; today, the usual thing is to buy it in décimos, one tenth of that billete.

At the official price, a décimo costs €20 and the full billete (the 10 décimos) €200. Since the terms —number, series, billete, décimo— are easily mixed up, it's worth confirming the equivalences with the lottery retailer before buying, so you take home exactly what you want.

One number, or several décimos spread out?

 

This is the real decision: concentrating your play on one number, or spreading it across several. They're two different ways to experience the draw, not two levels of probability.

→ One number, several décimos (or the whole billete): if that number wins, you collect on every décimo of it you hold. More concentrated: either a lot, or nothing.

→ Several numbers, one décimo of each: you spread your spend across different numbers. More diversified: more "horses in the race," but fewer décimos per number if one comes up.

The key point, and we won't tire of repeating it: every number has the same probability. Spreading out doesn't improve your mathematical odds; nor does it worsen them. If you want to understand that point well, we explain it in the odds of winning the Christmas Lottery.

So, what's best for you?

 

It depends on what you're after, not on what "gives better odds" (because they're the same). Two questions help you decide:

→ Do you want the "all or nothing" of a single number? Then concentrate: one number with several décimos, or the billete. If it lands, it lands big.

→ Would you rather be in on more numbers? Then spread out: décimos of different numbers. You don't gain any probability, but you play with your hopes spread across more of them.

There's no right answer: there's the one you fancy. Choose the one you'll enjoy, and always play within what you want to spend.

If you play a billete as a group

When several people share a number or a billete, the important thing is to make clear who holds what before the draw. Who puts in how much, whose name each décimo is under, and how a prize would be split. A clear written agreement avoids misunderstandings on 22 December.

What is a Christmas lottery billete?

Is it better to play one number or several?

For the probability, it makes no difference: every number has the same odds. Playing one number concentrates your play; spreading across several diversifies it. It's a matter of taste, not a winning strategy.

Does spreading across several numbers increase my chances?

No more than playing the same total on a single number. What changes is how you play: more numbers with fewer décimos each, or fewer numbers with more décimos. The probability of each number is identical.

How much does a whole billete cost?

Can I buy several décimos of the same number?

Yes, as long as there are décimos of that number available. That way you concentrate your play on one number without having to take the whole billete. Remember: every number has the same probability.
