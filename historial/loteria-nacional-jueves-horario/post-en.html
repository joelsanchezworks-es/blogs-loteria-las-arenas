The real window you have to buy your décimo

 

Here's the crux of the matter, the one that gives this guide its title. The draw is at 21:00, yes, but you can't buy right up until 21:00. Sales of décimos for that particular draw close a couple of hours earlier, usually around 19:00. That's your real window: you can buy for days, but to play that very night you have to do it before the cut-off.

What happens if you're late? You don't miss out on playing, but you play on a different day. Any décimo you buy after the cut-off is registered for the next draw, not that night's. In other words, if at 20:15 on Thursday you buy ‘for tonight’, you're actually buying for next Thursday. And that mix-up, when there's a lucky number involved, can prove very costly.

The advice, then, is simple: don't race the clock. Buy your Thursday décimo with time to spare — in the morning, at midday or early in the afternoon — and that way you make sure you're in the draw you want. And if you have a fixed number you always play, better still: leave it reserved or on a subscription so you're not relying on getting there in time every week.

A couple of nuances worth bearing in mind. First, the cut-off can be brought forward or shifted in weeks with public holidays or on special dates, so if the Thursday is the eve of something, check the time before you get complacent. Second, whether you buy at a physical lottery office or online, the logic is the same: there's a cut-off time for that draw, and once it passes the system no longer accepts décimos for that draw. In other words, the clock is the same for every channel, so buying online at five to nine won't fix a last-minute rush.

And the official count (escrutinio)? When your result becomes official

 

Once the draw has been held at 21:00, the results are known straight away, but it's worth remembering one detail: the result circulating in the moment is provisional. For it to be final it has to go through the escrutinio, the official process by which Loterías validates and confirms the list of prizes. It doesn't usually take long — just a short while after the draw — but it's the official result, not a screenshot, that counts when it comes to collecting.

In practice, for the Thursday draw this means you have the result the same night, but the sensible thing is to check it against the official list before celebrating (or dismissing) anything. We always check against the official result, not screenshots doing the rounds on social media.
