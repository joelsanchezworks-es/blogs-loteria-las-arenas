The quick answer

6 numbers from 1 to 49 · €0.50 a bet · a daily draw · prizes from 3 matches

To play Bonoloto you pick 6 numbers from 1 to 49 on each bet, which costs €0.50. There's a draw every day (seven draws a week). The draw produces 6 numbers, plus a complementary number and a reintegro. You win a prize from 3 matches upwards, and with the reintegro you can get your stake back.

How do you play Bonoloto? The rules step by step

 

Playing Bonoloto comes down to choosing 6 numbers from 1 to 49 and hoping they match the ones drawn. You don't pick the complementary number or the reintegro: those are generated on their own. Let's go through each part on its own, because that's where it all falls into place.

Choose your 6 numbers (or let the machine choose them)

On each bet you mark 6 numbers from 1 to 49 on the slip. If you'd rather not think them up, you can ask for a ‘random’ bet and the system picks them for you at random. You can play a single bet or several on the same slip; each 6-number bet costs €0.50.

The complementary number: the number that decides the second tier

The complementary number is a seventh number (from 1 to 49) drawn after the main six. You don't choose it: it's used to decide the second prize category. If you match 5 of the 6 numbers and, on top of that, one of your numbers matches the complementary number, you move up from the ‘5 matches’ tier to the ‘5 + complementary’ tier, which pays out quite a bit more.

The reintegro: getting back what you staked

The reintegro is a digit from 0 to 9 that's also drawn in the draw. Each bet is automatically assigned a number, and if its last figure matches the reintegro, you get the cost of that bet back (the €0.50). You don't choose it, and the odds are 1 in 10, so on average one in every ten bets returns your stake. It's the smallest prize… and the most frequent.

Price: how much it costs to play Bonoloto

 

Each Bonoloto bet costs €0.50, and that's the minimum bet. It's easily the cheapest daily lottery in the country. If you want to play more numbers or more combinations, you can fill in several bets on the same slip or enter several consecutive draws; the price simply multiplies by the number of bets.

That's one of Bonoloto's big advantages over other games: for very little money you're in a daily draw. There are no jackpots as enormous as EuroMillions', but you don't need to spend much to take part either, and that makes it the ideal choice for playing with your head screwed on and without denting your wallet.

Bonoloto's odds, explained step by step

 

The odds of matching all 6 Bonoloto numbers are 1 in 13,983,816, the same as in La Primitiva. It sounds impossible, and for the first category it very nearly is; but since you win from 3 matches, the chances of taking home *some* prize are far, far higher. Seeing it in a table helps it sink in:

Odds of a prize by category

The top category is exceptionally rare, but the reintegro (1 in 10) and 3 matches (1 in 57) are relatively frequent. No combination is more likely than any other: they all have exactly the same chance.

There are two sensible ways to read this table. On the one hand, don't get fixated on 6 matches: they're roughly one in almost fourteen million, and buying more combinations barely shifts that figure. On the other, enjoy what genuinely is within reach: getting your stake back with the reintegro or landing a 3- or 4-match prize happens often. Playing well means understanding this and not expecting miracles: Bonoloto is cheap daily fun, not an investment plan.

Frequently asked questions

 
How exactly do you play Bonoloto?

You pick 6 numbers from 1 to 49 on each bet, which costs €0.50. In the draw — held every day — 6 numbers, a complementary number and a reintegro are drawn. You win a prize from 3 matches upwards: the more numbers you match, the higher the category, up to all 6 for the first tier. The complementary number improves the 5-match category, and the reintegro returns the cost of your bet if its digit matches. You don't choose the complementary number or the reintegro yourself: they're generated automatically.

How much does it cost to play Bonoloto and which days are there draws?

Each bet costs €0.50, which is the minimum bet and the cheapest of the country's daily lotteries. There's a draw every day of the week (seven draws a week). You can play a single bet or several on the same slip, and also enter several consecutive draws; the price multiplies by the number of bets. Remember to buy with time to spare before the draw so you get into that same day's draw.

What are the odds of winning Bonoloto?

Matching all 6 numbers has odds of 1 in 13,983,816, the same as in La Primitiva. But since you win from 3 matches, the chances of taking home some prize are much greater: 3 matches is roughly 1 in 57, and the reintegro, 1 in 10. In other words, getting your stake back or landing a small prize is relatively frequent; the jackpot, exceptionally rare. And one important detail: every combination has exactly the same probability, so there are no numbers that are ‘better’ than others.

Is there a number to win the lottery today in 2026?

No, there isn't. Because Bonoloto is daily, plenty of people search for ‘today's winning number’, but it's a search doomed to fail: each draw is pure chance and independent of the last. Every combination from 1 to 49 has the same probability, and no method, hunch or website can know which balls will come out. Choose whatever numbers you fancy — by dates, at random or however you like — play only what you decide to spend and enjoy the game responsibly. Anyone who promises you ‘today's number for a guaranteed win’ is having you on.

How does the 1220 code work for winning?

It doesn't work. The ‘1220 code’ (or ‘1220 method’) is a numerology system doing the rounds on videos and social media that claims to deduce the next number from the last result. The problem is fundamental: it assumes the draws follow a pattern, and they don't. In Bonoloto, as in any lottery, each draw is independent and random, so no calculation based on yesterday's results can predict today's. It's entertainment, not a method, and it certainly doesn't justify spending more money chasing it.
