Curiosities from the history of the Gordo

 

Behind every number there is a story, and some are memorable:

→ The Gordo that fell on a single town: in 2011, the number 58268 was sold almost entirely in Grañén (Huesca), a municipality of around 2,000 inhabitants that took the whole first prize.

→ A very concentrated Gordo: in 2024, 72480 fell entirely in Logroño, shared out from a single lottery office.

→ Repeating, almost never: in more than two centuries of draws, the first-prize number has practically never come up again. Even so, that does not make any number 'due' to win.

They are great anecdotes to tell, but look at the real pattern: there is no pattern. The Gordo has fallen on high numbers, low ones, with zeros and without them, in towns and in cities.

Does history help you choose a number?

 

Here is the honest answer to the million-dollar question: no. Looking at the numbers that have come up in other years does nothing to improve your odds. In the Christmas Lottery, every number from 00000 to 99999 has exactly the same probability of being the Gordo, whether it has come up before or not.

Chance has no memory: the winners do not 'repeat', nor are the ones that have not come up 'due'. So choose your number for whatever makes you happy — a date, a whim, chance — and enjoy the draw, without chasing formulas that do not exist.

Where to check all the winning numbers

 

If you are curious about the complete history, the reliable source is the official one. The Sociedad Estatal Loterías y Apuestas del Estado (SELAE), the Spanish state lottery operator, keeps the record of all the Christmas draws, which have been held since 1812.

Be wary, on the other hand, of websites that promise 'hot numbers' or predictions based on the historical record: they are based on nothing. The draw is pure chance, and that is precisely its charm.
