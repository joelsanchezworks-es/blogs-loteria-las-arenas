How does the Lotería Nacional work? Number, series and décimos

 

The Lotería Nacional is organised into numbers, series, billetes and décimos, and it’s easier to grasp than it sounds. Each piece has its own name and job; we’ll explain them one by one so you know exactly what you’re buying.

The number and the series

The number is the five digits that identify your décimo, from 00000 to 99999. Several series are issued of each number — identical copies of it: in the ordinary Saturday draw, for example, around 10 series per number. The more series there are, the more décimos of that number exist, and the more people can share the thrill of playing it.

Billete, décimo and fracción

A complete series of a number forms a billete, which is divided into 10 décimos. The décimo is therefore a tenth of a billete — what you normally buy: in the ordinary Saturday draw it costs €6 (the whole billete, €60). In the big special draws, such as Christmas or El Niño, those décimos are called fracciones, but the idea is the same: a portion of the billete.

And there are two more ways to take part that are worth knowing. The first is participaciones (shares): instead of buying a whole décimo, you play a portion of it — very common among syndicates, bars, companies and families. With a share of, say, €3 or €5, you're in the same number as the rest of the group and you take the proportional part if it wins. It's the cheapest way into a draw, as long as the slip is stamped by an official lottery office and correctly shows the number, the amount and the draw. The second is the subscription (abono): reserving your number so we keep it for you draw after draw, without you having to come in every week to buy it. It's ideal if you always play the same number —a date, an ending— and don't want to risk being left without it the very week it comes up. In both cases, the prize mechanics are exactly the same as we've seen: participaciones and abonos don't change your odds or the categories, only the way you buy and share the décimo. The key, as always, is to be clear about what you're playing, with whom and for how much, and to keep the receipt or the slip safe until after the draw. A shared or subscribed décimo gives you the same thrill as one bought at the last minute — except that, playing it this way, it's much harder to forget it or to lose out on your number.

The reintegro: getting your décimo’s price back

The reintegro is the simplest way to win: it matches the last digit of the first prize. If your number ends in that same digit, you get the price of the décimo back. You don’t have to match anything else — just that last digit — so the odds are 1 in 10. It’s the smallest prize and, by far, the most frequent, so you should never throw away a décimo without at least checking the reintegro.

The prizes almost nobody knows about: aproximaciones, centenas and terminaciones

 

Aproximaciones, centenas and terminaciones are the “hidden” categories that make a lot of people winners without their knowing it. They sound technical, but you can grasp them in a minute, and knowing they exist is exactly what stops you throwing away a winning décimo. Here are the three:

→ Aproximaciones: these reward the numbers immediately before and after the first and second prize. If your number is next-door to the jackpot, you win.

→ Centenas: these reward the décimos in the same centena as the first or second prize — that is, the ones that share the first three digits (for example, from 45,800 to 45,899 if the prize is 45.8XX).

→ Terminaciones: these reward matches on the last digits of the first prize. The ending of the last two digits, for example, pays out €12 per décimo; the reintegro is the single-digit ending.

In practice, these categories hand out the most prizes by sheer number, even if the amounts are small. Most people who “haven’t won anything” simply haven’t looked beyond the whole number. A glance at the terminación and the reintegro changes that feeling more often than you’d think.

Frequently asked questions

 
How does the Lotería Nacional work?

Each décimo carries a five-digit number (from 00000 to 99999) already printed on it. You buy the décimo — you don’t choose the numbers — and take part in every category of the draw. Several series are issued for each number; a complete series is a billete, which is divided into 10 décimos. You win if your number matches the winning ones, in full or in part: first and second prize, aproximaciones, centenas, terminaciones and the reintegro. The ordinary draws are held on Thursdays and Saturdays.

How many prize categories are there in the Lotería Nacional?

Quite a few more than people think. As well as the first and second prize, there are aproximaciones (the numbers before and after the top prizes), centenas (the same centena as the 1st or 2nd prize), terminaciones (a match on the last digits of the first prize) and the reintegro (the last digit of the first prize, which gives you the price of the décimo back). With a single décimo you take part in all of them at once, so there are many ways to win something, even if you don’t land the jackpot.

What is the reintegro and how do you win it?

The reintegro matches the last digit of the first prize. If your number ends in that same digit, you get back the price of the décimo you paid. You don’t have to match anything else, and the odds are 1 in 10, so it’s the most frequent prize. It’s small — it gives you back your stake — but it’s always worth checking: plenty of people throw away décimos that had, at the very least, the reintegro.

Is there a number to win the lottery today, in 2026?

There isn’t one. In the Lotería Nacional the décimos already come with the number printed on them, and every one has exactly the same chance of being a winner: there’s no “magic” number and no trick for guessing today’s. The draw is pure chance, and no website, app or “expert” can know which number will come up. Choose the décimo that appeals to you — a date, a terminación you like, or whichever one you’re handed at the counter — and play responsibly. If anyone sells you “today’s winning number”, be suspicious: they’re taking you for a ride.

How does the 1220 code work for winning?

It doesn’t work in any way at all. The “1220 code” (or “1220 method”) is numerology doing the rounds on social media and in videos, claiming to predict the next number from the last result. It’s false at its root: it assumes the draws follow a pattern, and they don’t. Every Lotería Nacional draw is independent and random, and no calculation based on previous results can guess the next one. It’s a hook to pull in clicks, not a method; chasing it just makes you spend more than you should.
