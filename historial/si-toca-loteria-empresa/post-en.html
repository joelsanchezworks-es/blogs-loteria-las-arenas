What happens if the company's Christmas Lottery wins

If it wins, the first thing isn't to celebrate the split: it's knowing who owns each décimo (lottery ticket) or share. We explain how to verify the prize, where it is collected, what changes with safekeeping and how a shared prize is taxed.

If the company's Christmas Lottery wins, the first thing to do is check what each person has bought and who owns each décimo or share. Sharing the company's number doesn't necessarily mean sharing the same décimo.

Then you need to verify the prize against official sources, keep the documentation and follow the relevant collection procedure. How you bought matters: you must not mix up the instructions for a physical décimo, a purchase held in safekeeping and a share managed by another entity.

The key point

Sharing a number isn't sharing a décimo

Before splitting anything, clarify ownership: if each employee bought their own décimo, each one has an individual purchase, even though they all play the same number, and there is no single company prize for the whole workforce. Always verify the result against official sources, keep the documentation and follow the collection procedure for your type of purchase. And remember the deadline: the right to collect expires after 3 months.

Sharing a number isn't sharing a décimo

 

If each employee has bought their own décimo, each one has an individual purchase even though they all play the same number. For that reason there is no single company prize to be split across the whole workforce.

If several people share a décimo, you need to be able to prove who is taking part and in what proportion. If shares have been issued, they must be linked to the décimos that back them and to their organiser's instructions.

The fact that the company coordinated the campaign doesn't automatically make it the owner of the employees' purchases either. If it has made lottery purchases to give away or for itself, that situation and its actual ownership must be treated separately.

Check the prize and keep the documentation

 

Use the official information from Loterías y Apuestas del Estado (SELAE) to check the result of the draw. A forwarded message or a screenshot from social media is no substitute for that verification.

If you have a physical décimo, keep it in good condition. If you bought online, save the information and the receipt provided. If you are taking part through shares, check their identification and contact the organiser through the designated channel.

Don't share full photographs of décimos or receipts showing details that don't need to be made public. If a problem arises, use the official help channels and avoid sending passwords or full payment details.

Where décimos are collected and by when

 

According to SELAE, prizes under 2.000 € per décimo or receipt can be collected through its retail network. Those of 2.000 € or more are collected at authorised financial institutions, with the appropriate identification and documentation.

The threshold refers to the prize on the décimo or receipt, not to the amount that ultimately goes to each participant. Splitting a prize between several people doesn't turn it into several separate smaller prizes for these purposes.

SELAE also establishes that the right to collect expires after three months, counted from the day after the draw is held. Don't wait until the end of the deadline to clarify ownership or sort out problems.

What changes when the purchase is in safekeeping

 

In an online purchase with deposit at Las Arenas, the décimos are kept in safekeeping at our lottery office. To find out the steps that apply to a prize, check the procedure applicable to your purchase.

You shouldn't assume there is physical collection, automatic payment or a set transfer time unless it is confirmed in its terms. Nor should the instructions from the SELAE website or another platform be used as Las Arenas's instructions: they are different channels.

If the service has been arranged through a third party, identify them and follow their verified instructions, in addition to the rules that apply to the prize.

How the taxation of a shared prize works

 

For personal income tax (IRPF) payers, the legislation (Law 35/2006) sets an exempt amount of 40.000 € per winning décimo or bet and a 20 % charge on the excess. When a single décimo is shared, the exemption is distributed proportionally among its holders: a 40.000 € exemption does not apply to each person for their share in that same décimo.

The Spanish Tax Agency (Agencia Tributaria) explains that the beneficiaries and their percentages must be identified, and the split must be provable. Agree the necessary documentation with the paying entity before collecting.

If the prize belongs to the company

 

When the holder of the prize is a company, its treatment is not the same as that described for individuals. The Spanish Tax Agency (Agencia Tributaria) states that Corporate Income Tax (Impuesto sobre Sociedades) payers must include the prize in the period's income and treat the withholding borne as a payment on account.

For company prizes, gifts with uncertain ownership, non-resident participants or disagreements over the split, consult a tax or legal adviser about your case. General information is no substitute for that review.

Frequently asked questions about a company lottery prize

 
If we all play the same number, is there a single company prize?

Not necessarily. If each employee bought their own décimo, each one has an individual purchase even though they all play the same number, and there is no single company prize to share out among the workforce. Only when several people share a single décimo do you need to prove who is taking part and in what proportion. Sharing the company's number is not the same as sharing a décimo.

Where is a company lottery prize collected?

It depends on the amount per décimo. According to SELAE, prizes under 2.000 € per décimo or receipt can be collected through its retail network; those of 2.000 € or more are collected at authorised financial institutions, with the appropriate identification and documentation. The threshold refers to the prize on the décimo, not to what ultimately goes to each participant: splitting a prize doesn't turn it into several smaller prizes.

How long do you have to collect the prize?

The right to collect expires after three months, counted from the day after the draw is held. It's best not to wait until the end of the deadline to clarify ownership or sort out problems: the first thing is to verify the prize against official sources and, if the décimo is shared or there is any doubt over ownership, to resolve it as soon as possible.

How is a prize shared between several employees taxed?

For personal income tax (IRPF), there is 40.000 € exempt per winning décimo and 20 % on the excess. When a décimo is shared, that exemption is split proportionally among the holders (it isn't 40.000 € for each one). For example, on a 100.000 € prize in five equal parts: 40.000 € exempt, 60.000 € at 20 % (12.000 € of withholding), 88.000 € net, that is 17.600 € per person. The Spanish Tax Agency (Agencia Tributaria) asks that the beneficiaries and their percentages be identified and that the split be provable.

What happens if the décimo is bought with deposit and safekeeping?

The décimos are kept in safekeeping at the lottery office, and for a prize you have to follow the procedure applicable to that purchase. You shouldn't assume there is physical collection, automatic payment or a specific transfer time unless it is confirmed in the terms. And Las Arenas's instructions are not replaced by those of the SELAE website or those of another platform: they are different channels.

And what if the lottery was in the company's name?

If the holder of the prize is a company, its treatment is not that of an individual: the Spanish Tax Agency (Agencia Tributaria) states that Corporate Income Tax (Impuesto sobre Sociedades) payers include the prize in the period's income and treat the withholding borne as a payment on account. In these cases — and also with gifts of uncertain ownership, non-residents or disagreements over the split — the prudent course is to consult a tax or legal adviser.
