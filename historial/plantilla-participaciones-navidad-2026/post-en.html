Frequently asked questions about the Christmas shares template

 
What is a Christmas Lottery shares template?

It's a simple document where you put in writing who is taking part in a shared décimo and for how much. It captures the number, the draw, the lottery office, the price of the décimo, the list of participants with their contribution and share, the organiser and the signatures. It's not an official contract: it's a clear sheet so the group can play without misunderstandings.

What details can't be missing from the template?

The number and the draw, the lottery office where it was bought, the price of the décimo, the list of participants with what each person contributes, each person's share (%) (which must add up to 100 %), the organiser who keeps the décimo and a space for date and signatures. With that, the split is clear in advance.

How are the shares calculated?

Each person's share is the proportion of what they contribute relative to the total. If the décimo costs €20 and someone puts in €5, their share is 25 %. The important thing is that the contributions add up to the price of the décimo and that the percentages add up to 100 %.

Do you need to sign the template?

It's not compulsory, but it's highly recommended. The signatures (or, failing that, sharing the template with the whole group) give the agreement validity and prevent arguments. Ideally you fill it in and sign it before the draw, not after, and keep it together with the proof of purchase.

Where do I download the template?

You can download it free as a PDF from this article, ready to print. Print one template for each décimo the group is going to share, fill it in with the details of the décimo and the participants, and keep it with the proof of purchase.

What happens if our group wins?

The prize is divided according to the shares written down. Lottery prizes have a tax-exempt portion (€40,000) and are taxed at 20 % on the rest; with a shared prize, the usual thing is to identify all the participants when claiming so that each person's part applies to them. This is general information; for your specific case, check with the lottery office or an adviser.
