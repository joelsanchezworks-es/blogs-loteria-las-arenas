'How much do you win per décimo?' is December's star question, and it has two answers: what the prize says… and what you actually keep. Because the big prizes go through the tax authorities (Hacienda) before reaching your account.

In the Christmas Lottery, the prize is split per décimo (what you buy for €20) and per series (the full ticket (billete), which is 10 décimos). Here you will see both figures and, most importantly, the net amount after tax.

In brief

€400,000 the Gordo… and €328,000 net

The Gordo pays out €400,000 per décimo (€4,000,000 per series). But lottery prizes have the first €40,000 tax-free and are taxed at 20 % on the rest. So, from the Gordo you keep €328,000 net per décimo. Prizes of €20,000 or less (the 4th, the 5th and the pedrea — the many small prizes) are completely tax-free: they are paid out in full.

How much do you win from the Christmas Gordo per décimo?

 

The Christmas Gordo pays out €400,000 for each winning décimo (gross). Since each number is divided into 10 décimos per series, the full series (the full ticket (billete)) adds up to €4,000,000.

If you share a décimo in a peña (a group), that prize is split among the participants according to their share. And if you play a participación (a share) of, say, €5, you collect the proportional part. In every case, the starting point is the prize per décimo: €400,000 for the Gordo.

What you keep after tax

 

Lottery prizes (SELAE) have one part that is tax-free and another that is taxed. The rule, as things stand today, is simple: the first €40,000 of each prize is exempt and a 20 % withholding applies to anything above that amount. That withholding is already deducted when you collect.

This means that prizes of up to €40,000 are paid out in full: the 4th prize (€20,000), the 5th (€6,000), the pedrea (€1,000) and the reintegro (refund) pay nothing. Only the Gordo, the 2nd and the 3rd are taxed. Here is the net:

Frequently asked questions about how much you win per décimo in the Christmas Lottery

 
How much do you win from the Christmas Gordo per décimo?

The Gordo pays out €400,000 per décimo gross (€4,000,000 for the full series, which is 10 décimos). After tax, with the first €40,000 exempt and 20 % on the rest, you keep €328,000 net per décimo.

What is the difference between the prize per décimo and per series?

The décimo is what you buy for €20, and its prize is the one usually announced 'per décimo'. The series is the full ticket (billete), made up of 10 décimos of the same number; its prize is the décimo's prize multiplied by 10. For example, the Gordo is €400,000 per décimo and €4,000,000 per series.

How much do you pay the tax authorities (Hacienda) on a Christmas Lottery prize?

The first €40,000 of each prize is exempt and 20 % is withheld on anything above that figure. The withholding is deducted automatically when you collect. So, a prize of up to €40,000 is paid out in full, and from the Gordo (€400,000) €72,000 is withheld, leaving €328,000 net.

Which prizes are paid out in full, with no tax?

All those of €40,000 or less: the 4th prize (€20,000), the 5th (€6,000), the pedrea (€1,000), the smaller approximations and the reintegro (refund). Only the three largest prizes are taxed: the Gordo, the 2nd and the 3rd, and only on the part that exceeds €40,000.

If I share the décimo, how does it affect the tax?

In a shared prize (a peña or participaciones), both the prize and the €40,000 exemption are split in proportion to each person's share. That is why it is worth identifying all the participants when you collect. This is general information: for your specific case, check our tax article or ask an adviser.

Do all numbers have the same probability of winning the Gordo?

Yes. Every number, from 00000 to 99999, has exactly the same probability of winning a prize. The prize amount does not depend on the number you choose, but on the category that comes up. There are no numbers that 'pay more' or 'bring more luck'.
