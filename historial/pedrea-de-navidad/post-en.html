The Christmas pedrea: what it is, how much it pays and what odds it has

We all dream of el Gordo (the top prize), but the prize that really gets shared out on 22 December goes by another name: the pedrea (the Christmas Lottery's shower of small prizes). We'll tell you what it is, how much it pays per décimo (a tenth-share of a ticket) and what odds it has, without the hype.

We all dream of el Gordo, but what the Christmas Lottery really hands out goes by another name: the pedrea. The pedrea is the 1,794 prizes of €1,000 per series — in other words, €100 per décimo that keep falling during the draw. It won't change your life, but it is, by far, what gets handed out the most.

That's why so many people 'win something' every 22 December even if the fortune doesn't land on them. Here's the clear explanation: what the pedrea is exactly, how much it pays, how it differs from the reintegro (refund of the stake) and what odds it has.

In brief

The pedrea, in one sentence

The pedrea is 1,794 prizes of €1,000 per series (€100 per décimo): the most widely shared prize of the Christmas Lottery Draw. It isn't taxed (it's well below the €40,000 above which Hacienda, the Spanish tax office, withholds). And note: it isn't the same as the reintegro, which only gives you back the €20 of the décimo.

What is the Christmas pedrea?

 

The 'pedrea' is the set of smaller prizes in the Christmas Lottery Draw: those 1,794 numbers that, without being el Gordo or the big prizes, receive €1,000 per series. It gets its name, like a shower of little stones, because it rains down on so many numbers at once.

It's the most 'democratic' part of the draw: it hands out small amounts, but among a huge number of people. If you've ever 'won something' at Christmas and it wasn't a huge amount, it was almost certainly the pedrea.

Because it falls on so many numbers, it's a good excuse to get into the draw: you can buy the Christmas Lottery online and play for the pedrea too, not just for el Gordo.

How much does the pedrea pay? (per series and per décimo)

 

Each pedrea prize is €1,000 per series, which works out to €100 per décimo. Since a décimo costs €20, if the pedrea lands on you, you get your décimo back and walk away with €80 of clean profit. That's nice, but it helps to keep it in perspective:

The reintegro isn't the pedrea: it only gives back your stake

Many people confuse the pedrea and the reintegro, and they're not the same thing. The reintegro rewards the décimos whose last digit matches that of el Gordo (and that of other prizes), and all it does is give you back the €20 you paid. You don't win: you just get your money back. The pedrea, on the other hand, does leave a profit (€100 per décimo). If you want the full map of prizes, we explain it in how the Christmas Lottery prizes are shared out.

What are the odds of the pedrea?

 

The pedrea rewards 1,794 of the 100,000 numbers in the drum, so the chance that your number receives a pedrea prize is around 1.8%. It is, by far, the prize most likely to 'come up', far more than el Gordo.

But watch out for a very common mental trap: winning the pedrea isn't 'winning the lottery'. Most of the time we're talking about getting your stake back or a small profit. And, as in any draw, every number has exactly the same probability: neither the palindromes, nor the 'pretty' ones, nor the ones ending in 5 have more chances than yours.

Frequently asked questions about the pedrea of the Christmas Lottery

 
What is the pedrea of the Christmas Lottery?

It's the set of smaller prizes in the Christmas Lottery Draw: 1,794 numbers that receive €1,000 per series (€100 per décimo). It's called the pedrea because it falls on a huge number of tickets at once, like a shower of little stones. It's the most widely shared prize of 22 December.

How much do you win with the pedrea?

€100 per décimo (€1,000 per series). Since the décimo costs €20, you get back what you paid and win €80 net. It isn't taxed, because it's well below the €40,000 above which the Spanish tax office (Hacienda) withholds.

Are the pedrea and the reintegro the same?

No. The reintegro only gives you back the €20 of the décimo (when your number ends in the same digit as el Gordo). The pedrea does leave a profit: €100 per décimo. They're two different things and can happen separately.

What are the chances of winning the pedrea?

Around 1.8% per number (1,794 prizes out of 100,000 numbers). It's the easiest prize to win in the draw, though it's still modest. Remember: every number has the same probability.

Does the pedrea pay tax?

No. Christmas Lottery prizes are only taxed above €40,000, and the pedrea (€100 per décimo) is well below that. You collect it in full, with no withholding.
