What is El Gordo de la Primitiva and how does it differ from La Primitiva?

 

El Gordo de la Primitiva and La Primitiva are two different lotteries, even if their names look alike. The draw day changes, how many numbers you pick changes, the price of the bet changes and even the jackpot changes. Sharing a surname doesn't mean sharing the rules.

What time is the Sunday draw?

 

El Gordo de la Primitiva is drawn on Sunday evenings, in a single weekly draw. As with every other lottery, sales close a little while before the draw, so to play that same Sunday you need to place your bet in good time and not leave it to the last minute.

The golden rule is the usual one: the time that really affects you isn't the draw time but the sales cut-off. Confirm the exact time at your lottery office or on the official channel, especially around special dates, and play with time to spare. If you tend to play the same number every week, the easiest thing is to leave it reserved or on subscription so you don't have to rely on getting there in time every Sunday.

How El Gordo de la Primitiva's official count (escrutinio) works

 

The official count (escrutinio) is the official process by which, after the Sunday draw, the winning combination is validated, the winners in each prize category are counted and the prizes are calculated. Until that process finishes, the result going around is provisional; only when the count concludes is the result official, and it's that result the prizes are paid on.

What exactly is the official count?

It's the draw's 'quality control'. Once the 5 numbers and the key number have been drawn, Loterías (the Spanish State Lottery) checks the combination, counts how many tickets have matched in each of the prize categories and works out the amounts due. El Gordo puts a fixed percentage of its sales revenue towards prizes, so the split depends on how much has been staked and how many winners there are.

The official count, step by step

1

The winning combination is validated

The 5 numbers and the key number drawn in the Sunday draw are officially confirmed.

2

Winners are counted by category

From the top category (5 + key number) down to the lowest, the number of winning tickets in each is counted.

3

The amounts are calculated

The prize fund is shared out among the categories and their winners. The first prize takes the jackpot; if there are no first-category winners, the jackpot rolls over to the following Sunday.

4

The official result is published

Once the official count is closed, the official prize list is published. That's the result that counts for all purposes.

When is the result official?

Shortly after the draw, but not the instant the balls come out. The result you see straight away on social media or apps is provisional; the official one is the result that has been through the official count. It usually doesn't take long, so the advice is simple: don't celebrate or write anything off on the strength of a screenshot; wait for the official result and check it calmly.

Provisional isn't official. What goes around on social media right after the Sunday draw is a heat-of-the-moment snapshot that can contain errors. To collect on — or to write off — a ticket, trust only the official result from the count.

El Gordo's jackpot: why it isn't the same as La Primitiva's

 

El Gordo de la Primitiva's jackpot is its own and rolls over on its own account: it has nothing to do with La Primitiva's jackpot. They're two different piggy banks, each with its own game. If nobody matches El Gordo's first category on a given Sunday, that jackpot grows for the following Sunday; La Primitiva's jackpot goes its own separate way, with the Thursday and Saturday draws.

Here's one of the most expensive mix-ups. A lot of people hear 'the Primitiva jackpot' on the news and assume there's just one, when in reality there are two independent jackpots: La Primitiva's and El Gordo's. They can both be high at once, or one high and the other low, because they don't talk to each other. El Gordo, what's more, always starts from a guaranteed minimum of €5 million, so it's never drawn for less than that figure. So if you're going for the Sunday jackpot, your bet has to be on El Gordo de la Primitiva, not on Saturday's La Primitiva.

Frequently asked questions

 
How does El Gordo de la Primitiva differ from La Primitiva?

They're two different games. La Primitiva is drawn on Thursday and Saturday, you pick 6 numbers from 1 to 49 plus the reintegro (refund number) and the bet costs €1. El Gordo de la Primitiva is drawn on Sundays, you pick 5 numbers from 1 to 54 plus a key number (from 0 to 9) and the bet costs €1.50. On top of that, each one has its own jackpot, independent of the other. They share part of their name, but not the rules or the draws, so a ticket for one doesn't play in the other.

What time is the El Gordo de la Primitiva draw?

El Gordo de la Primitiva is drawn on Sunday evenings, in a single weekly draw. As with any lottery, sales close a little while before the draw, so to play that Sunday you need to buy in good time. The time that really matters is the sales cut-off, not the draw itself: confirm it at your lottery office or on the official channel and don't leave it to the last minute.

How is El Gordo de la Primitiva's official count done?

The official count is the official process that, after the Sunday draw, validates the combination, counts the winners in each category and calculates the prizes. First the 5 numbers and the key number are confirmed; then the winning tickets are counted by category; next the prize fund is shared out (the first prize takes the jackpot, which rolls over if there are no winners); and finally the official list is published. Until that process finishes, the result is provisional: the one that counts for collecting is the official one.

Is there a number to win the lottery today in 2026?

No. There's no guaranteed 'winning' number, not in 2026 or in any other year. Every draw is pure chance: all combinations have exactly the same probability and one day's result has no bearing on the next, because chance has no memory. Anyone who promises you 'the number to win today' is selling you snake oil. The sensible thing is to pick the combination that appeals to you, play only what you want to spend and enjoy the game responsibly, without expecting miracles.

How does the 1220 code work for winning?

The '1220 code' (or '1220 method') is a numerology system doing the rounds on social media and in videos, which promises to deduce the next number from the last result. Put plainly: it doesn't work, because it starts from a false idea — that draws follow a pattern. They don't. Each draw is independent and random, so no calculation based on past results can predict the future. It's entertainment, not a method; and it certainly doesn't justify spending more money. In games of chance there are no secret formulas, however convincing the video sounds.
