Christmas Lottery endings over the last 10 years

 

Let’s get to the fact you came here for, told properly. Across the entire history of El Gordo, the ending that has come up most is the 5: it has crowned the first prize 32 times. Next come the 4 and the 6, with 27 times each. At the other end, the least fortunate have been the 1 (8 times), the 2 (13) and the 9 (16). These are real figures, quoted every Christmas.

But here comes the contrast that changes everything. If instead of the whole history we look only at the last 10 years (2016-2025), the famous 5 hasn’t shown up even once as an El Gordo ending. The historical star has been missing for a full decade. Coincidence? No: it’s exactly what you’d expect from chance.

Here they are, one by one, the El Gordo endings of the past decade: 2016 → 3, 2017 → 8, 2018 → 7, 2019 → 0, 2020 → 7, 2021 → 8, 2022 → 0, 2023 → 8, 2024 → 0 and 2025 → 2. In short: the 0 and the 8 came up most (3 times each), the 7 twice, the 2 and the 3 once, and the 1, 4, 5, 6 and 9 don’t appear at all.

Let’s run the probability contrast. In theory, each ending has a 10% chance of coming up, so across 10 draws it ‘should’ land once per digit. The reality: some have come up three times and others five, zero. Does that mean the 0 and the 8 are ‘hot’? No. Ten draws are a tiny sample, and that unevenness is pure chance, not a trend. The best proof is the 5 itself: the most awarded in history and, even so, zero appearances in the last decade. If the draw had a memory, someone would say the 5 ‘is due’ for having gone so long without coming up; but that’s exactly the gambler’s fallacy. The drum owes nothing to anyone: next year, the 5 goes back to its 10%, just like the 0 that’s so in fashion right now.

The conclusion is twofold and worth repeating: there are no luckier endings for 2026, and no track record —neither two centuries of it nor the last ten years— helps predict the next one. Every 22 December, the ten digits start again from zero with the same 10%.

So if it makes no difference to winning, why does the ending matter so much? Because it matters for getting hold of it. And there a practical, very real fact kicks in: every year, the same endings are the first to vanish from the lottery offices.

If your ending is one of those, waiting until December is the surest way to miss out on it. The most common mistakes with endings

 

None of these mistakes is about choosing the wrong ending —they’re all equally likely. They have to do with beliefs and with timing. These are the ones we see most:

⚠ Believing an ending is ‘due’

It’s the gambler’s fallacy. The fact that a digit hasn’t come up for years doesn’t make it any more likely: the draw has no memory. Choosing by ‘whose turn it is’ improves nothing.

⚠ Betting everything on the 5

The 5 is the most frequent ending in history, yes, but that’s the past. In 2026 it has exactly the same probability as the 0 or the 9. Playing it out of tradition is fine; playing it for an ‘edge’ is not.

⚠ Waiting until December for a popular ending

If you’re after one of the most requested digits, leaving it to the last minute is the surest way to end up without it. The popular ones sell out much earlier.

⚠ Playing on superstition with no budget

Chasing ‘the lucky ending’ by buying more than you meant to doesn’t change the odds, only your wallet. Decide how much you want to play and enjoy it for what it is: a bit of excitement.

⚠ Not reserving your usual number

If you have ‘your’ lifelong ending, don’t leave it at the mercy of the December rush. Reserving it or becoming a subscriber is what guarantees it stays yours every year.
