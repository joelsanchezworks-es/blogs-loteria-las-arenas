When can you buy the 2026 Christmas Lottery?

 

The official on-sale date for the Christmas Draw décimos comes each summer, as soon as Loterías y Apuestas del Estado (the Spanish State Lottery) distributes the print run among the lottery offices. For the 2026 draw, that date was 29 June 2026. From that day, any authorised lottery office can issue décimos, and the sale stays open — unless a number sells out sooner — until 21 December, the eve of the draw.

Put another way: you have almost six months' leeway to buy, but not six months to choose your number. That's what throws a lot of people off. The sale does run until December, yes, but the specific numbers are limited: for each number there is only a fixed total of décimos spread across the whole of Spain, and once your favourite ending runs out, that's it. That's why, in practice, the Christmas campaign is decided long before the December bank-holiday weekend.

The risk of reserving your number over WhatsApp without a receipt

 

We come to the part that inspired half this guide. Every year it's the same story: someone wants to 'grab' a number before it sells out, messages an acquaintance, a group, or a private individual on WhatsApp, and gets a comfortable 'don't worry, I'll hold it for you'. They feel perfectly reassured… until December arrives.

The problem is easy to understand: a WhatsApp message is not a custody receipt. It's a conversation. If that number wins and the other person doesn't show up, changes their mind, or simply 'didn't remember' it was yours, you have no way to prove that décimo was reserved in your name. The excitement turns into a problem — and between people who knew each other, at that.

And we're not just talking about bad faith, though that exists too. We're talking about the most human of slip-ups: the person who jotted your reservation in a notebook that got lost, the one who accidentally sold the same number twice, the group where nobody knows any more who put in what. Without a document — physical or digital — that says 'this décimo belongs to this person', everything is left up in the air.

The difference between an informal reservation and a watertight one is best seen in a list. Take a look:

✗'I'll hold it for you' over WhatsApp: there's only a message; nothing certifies the décimo is yours if it wins.

✗A verbal reservation in a group: nobody signs anything and responsibility gets diluted among everyone.

✗Paying a private individual up front with no receipt: you hand over the money with no proof and no number assigned.

✓A reservation with a custody receipt: a document with your name, the number, and the lottery office backing it.

✓A décimo held in custody at an official lottery office: the décimo is put in your name and they notify you if it wins.

Décimo, billete, series and participación: exactly what you're buying

 

Before you reserve, it's worth being clear on the vocabulary, because not everyone buys the same thing. In the Christmas Lottery, the basic unit is the décimo, which costs 20 €. Each number has several series, and each series is divided into ten décimos — hence the name. Buying the ten décimos of a single series means buying a complete billete (a full ticket).

If 20 € feels like a lot for a single number, there are participaciones (fractional shares): a fraction of a décimo shared out via share slips, very typical among clubs, companies, bars, and families. It's the way to play more numbers for less money, as long as the share slip is stamped by an official lottery office and shows the details correctly.
