Finding a winning Niño décimo in a drawer… and discovering that it has already expired is one of the most avoidable disappointments there is. Lottery prizes cannot be claimed forever: you have a deadline, and once it passes, the money is lost.

The good news is that the rules are clear and the same for Christmas and for the Niño. In this guide we explain how long you have to claim, how much Hacienda really withholds and how and where to claim your prize with every guarantee.

An honest note: this is general information, not tax or legal advice. For very large prizes, shared décimos or inheritances, the prudent thing is to also consult a professional.

In brief

3 months to claim, exempt up to 40.000 €, 20% on the rest

You have 3 months from the day after the draw to claim a Niño prize (for the draw of 6 January 2027, until early April). Hacienda leaves the first 40.000 € exempt and withholds 20% on whatever exceeds that amount. Prizes of up to 2.000 € are claimed at the lottery office; larger ones, at a collaborating bank. The same deadline and the same taxation apply to the Christmas Lottery.

How long you have to claim: the 3-month deadline

 

Winning décimos expire after three months, counted from the day after the draw. For the Niño draw of 6 January 2027, that means you would have until roughly early April 2027 to claim.

If the deadline passes without your having claimed, the prize is lost and the amount goes to the Public Treasury. There are no extensions or exceptions for having forgotten it: that is why a décimo kept and unchecked is a real risk.

This deadline is identical to that of the Christmas Lottery. So whether you are wondering how long you have to claim the Christmas Lottery or the Niño, the answer is the same: three months from the day after the draw.

How much Hacienda withholds: the taxation of the prize

 

Not all of the prize is taxed. Prizes from the National Lottery carry a special levy with an exempt portion of 40.000 €; only what exceeds that figure is taxed, at a rate of 20%. In addition, the withholding is applied at source: the paying body (the lottery office or the bank) hands you the money with the 20% already deducted.

How and where to claim: the lottery office and collaborating banks

 

Where you claim depends on the amount of the prize. Prizes of up to 2.000 € per décimo can be claimed at the lottery office itself. For larger amounts, payment is made through a collaborating bank of the National Lottery.

In both cases you need the original décimo in good condition and your identity document. It is advisable to sign the back of the décimo and keep it without folding or damaging it: it is the proof of the prize, and if it is lost or seriously damaged, claiming it can become very complicated.

If you bought with deposit and safekeeping, you do not have to worry about the paper: the décimo is stored and checked, and the claim is handled with the guarantees of an official lottery office.

Prize safekeeping: don't let it expire in a drawer

 

The worst ending for a winning décimo is for no one to check it in time. It happens more than you might think: décimos that get misplaced, that are put away 'in a safe place' and forgotten, or that turn up when the deadline has already passed.

The simplest way to avoid it is not to depend on a piece of paper in a drawer. With deposit and safekeeping at an official lottery office, your décimo is stored, checked after the draw and kept safe until it is claimed. If there is a prize, they let you know; if not, you spare yourself the risk of losing it through carelessness.

Shared décimos and inheritances: how to act

 

When a décimo belongs to several people or is part of an inheritance, it is worth being clear on the rules before claiming. With a shared décimo, the important thing is that all participants are listed as joint owners of the prize at the time of claiming: this way each one is taxed on their share and the exempt portion of 40.000 € is split in proportion to their participation, preventing the split from being treated as a gift.

If the décimo appears in an inheritance (for example, a winning décimo from a deceased relative), it forms part of the estate and its claim must be handled by the heirs proving that status. These are situations in which the 3-month deadline keeps running, so it is best not to delay: the first thing is to check the décimo and, in parallel, to resolve the legal side.

Because of how complex these cases can be (several heirs, minors, non-residents…), here we do recommend relying on a lawyer or adviser. The good part: if the décimo was in safekeeping and checked, you at least avoid the risk of it expiring while everything else is being resolved.

Frequently asked questions about claiming the Lotería del Niño

 
How long do you have to claim the Lotería del Niño?

You have 3 months from the day after the draw. For the Niño draw of 6 January 2027, the deadline runs until roughly early April 2027. Once that deadline passes, the prize expires and the amount goes to the Public Treasury, with no extensions. The deadline is the same for the Christmas Lottery.

How much does Hacienda withhold on a Niño prize?

The first 40.000 € are exempt; on whatever exceeds that amount a 20% applies, withheld at source (you already receive the money with the 20% deducted). For example, on a prize of 100.000 €: 40.000 € exempt, 60.000 € as the base, 12.000 € withheld and 88.000 € net. For shared décimos, the exempt portion of 40.000 € is split according to each person's share.

Where do you claim a Lotería del Niño prize?

It depends on the amount. Prizes of up to 2.000 € per décimo are claimed at the lottery office. Larger ones are claimed through a collaborating bank of the National Lottery. In both cases you need the original décimo in good condition and your DNI (ID) or identity document.

What happens if I find a winning décimo that has already expired?

If more than 3 months have passed since the day after the draw, the prize can no longer be claimed: it has expired and the amount has gone to the Public Treasury. There are no extensions for forgetfulness. That is why it is so important to check décimos in time and, better still, to keep them in safekeeping and checked so that they do not depend on a piece of paper in a drawer.

How do you claim a shared or inherited décimo?

With a shared décimo, all participants must be listed as joint owners of the prize when claiming it, so that each one is taxed on their share and the 40.000 € exemption is split according to their participation. If the décimo forms part of an inheritance, it is included in the estate and is handled by the heirs proving that status. As the 3-month deadline keeps running, it is advisable to check the décimo as soon as possible and, for the legal side, to rely on a lawyer or adviser.

How do I keep a prize from expiring on me?

The safest way is to not depend on a stored piece of paper. With deposit and safekeeping at an official lottery office, your décimo is stored and checked after the draw, and they let you know if there is a prize. This way you eliminate the risk of misplacing it or forgetting it until it is too late. If you keep it yourself, note down the date of the draw and the claim deadline, and check the number as soon as possible.
