One euro, seven digits and fixed prizes from €1 to a million

The Joker is a random seven-digit number you add to La Primitiva for €1. You win if it matches the drawn Joker number, reading from the left or from the right: one digit pays €1 and all seven in order pay €1,000,000. It has negative expected value, like every lottery, but also a 19% chance of winning your euro back. Is it worth it? As a bit of extra entertainment, it can be; as an investment, no. Never tick it on autopilot without knowing what it is.

What the Joker is and how it works

 

The Joker is a game linked to La Primitiva that has been running since 2012: for €1 more on top of your bet, the system assigns you a completely random seven-digit number that you cannot choose. It is drawn a few minutes after La Primitiva, and it is entirely independent: you can win on the Joker even if you miss every single La Primitiva number, and the other way round.

The winning Joker number also has seven digits, and you win according to how many match in an unbroken run, starting from the left or from the right. The interesting part is that a single receipt can collect on both ends at once, and the prizes are added together. For example, if the winning number is 6306097 and yours is 6301597, you match the first three digits (€50) and the last two (€5): you collect €55 in total.

There's one key detail: the Joker has no rollover (no jackpot build-up). The prizes are fixed and do not accumulate from one draw to the next; if nobody matches the million, that million simply isn't paid out that week, but the amount is exactly the same the following week. Neither the money staked nor the number of winners changes what you collect.

A practical note: the Joker is marked in its own box on the La Primitiva slip — or activated when you play online — and is paid for only once per ticket, not for each column. In other words, even if you fill in several La Primitiva lines on the same receipt, the Joker euro is a single charge and gives you just one seven-digit number. That makes it relatively cheap within a large ticket and, proportionally, more expensive if you only play a single simple line.

Is the extra euro worth it?

 

The honest answer is: it depends on what you're after, but never play it expecting to make money in the long run. Like every lottery, the Joker pays out less in prizes than it takes in, so, on average, each euro you stake comes back in part, not in full. That said, there are arguments for and against that are worth weighing up.

The points in its favour

The Joker has two genuine attractions. The first: you win your euro back very easily, in almost 1 of every 5 bets, because all it takes is matching the first or the last digit. That sense that 'something keeps landing' is what hooks people. The second: the top prize, a million euros, carries odds of 1 in 10 million, which is actually a shade better than matching all six La Primitiva numbers (1 in almost 14 million). For a euro, you buy yourself a second shot at a big prize, with a number you don't even have to think about.

There's a third point, more psychological than mathematical: the Joker asks nothing of you. You don't pick digits, there are no superstitions and no calculations; the terminal hands you a number and that's that. For anyone who enjoys the draw without wanting to overthink it, that simplicity is part of the charm, and because the small prize lands so often, it keeps the excitement alive from one draw to the next.

The points against

On the other side of the scales sits the opportunity cost. That euro doubles the price of a simple La Primitiva line (from €1 to €2), and bet after bet, week after week, the bill mounts up. Anyone who plays the Joker all year round spends a far from trivial sum on it in exchange for an average return that, as with any lottery, is negative. On top of that, the million-euro prize is fixed and has no rollover: it neither grows nor accumulates, unlike La Primitiva's, which can shoot up during rollover weeks. If dreaming of really big figures is your thing, La Primitiva wins hands down there.

Let's put it in the perspective of a year: ticking the Joker on every La Primitiva draw, week after week, easily turns into over €100 a year on that extra euro alone. In return you'll win some of it back — those odd euros from when you match one or two digits — but, on average, you'll come out behind, because that's how it's designed. It's no disaster if that spending fits within your entertainment budget; it is one if you play it convinced that, by sheer persistence, it will eventually turn a profit. It won't.

The Joker has no rollover: the million is always a million. Don't wait for 'special weeks' with an accumulated prize, because there's no such thing. And remember that the extra euro is added to each bet: if you play several columns, the Joker is paid for only once per ticket, but it's worth bearing in mind when you work out what you really spend on each draw.

Joker vs La Primitiva: how they're alike and how they aren't

 

Although they travel on the same ticket, they are two different games. Understanding their differences is the best way to decide whether you want both or just one:

✓The Joker rewards getting money back: almost 1 of every 5 bets returns at least the euro. La Primitiva 'pays out' far less often.

✓The Joker's million is more 'reachable' (1 in 10 million) than La Primitiva's six-match prize (1 in almost 14 million).

✗But the Joker has no rollover: its top prize is always €1,000,000, whereas La Primitiva's can build up into the tens of millions.

✗The Joker doesn't improve your La Primitiva: it's a separate draw, with its own random number; it doesn't change your chances with the six numbers you picked one bit.

✗Both have negative expected value: neither is an investment. They're played for the thrill of it, with an entertainment budget.

Frequently asked questions about the Joker in La Primitiva

 
What is the Joker in La Primitiva?

It's an optional extra you can add to any La Primitiva bet for €1 more. It consists of a randomly generated seven-digit number chosen by the system (you don't pick it) that is drawn a few minutes after La Primitiva. It pays fixed prizes ranging from €1 to €1,000,000 depending on how many digits you match in a row. It's independent: you can win on the Joker even if you don't match a single La Primitiva number, and vice versa. It has been running since 2012.

How much can you win with the Joker, and with what odds?

There are seven tiers. Matching 1 digit (the first or the last) pays €1, with odds of close to 19%; 2 digits, €5; 3 digits, €50; 4 digits, €300; 5 digits, €1,000; 6 digits, €10,000; and all 7 digits in order, €1,000,000, with odds of 1 in 10 million. The matched digits must be consecutive, from the start or the end of the number, and you can collect on both ends at once.

Is it worth paying the Joker's extra euro?

It depends on why you play. As entertainment, it has its charm: for €1 you add a second flutter of hope and win your euro back in almost 1 of every 5 bets. As an investment, no: like every lottery, the Joker has negative expected value — that is, on average it returns less than it costs. On top of that, the euro doubles the price of a simple La Primitiva line, and playing it all year round the bill climbs. The sensible decision is to tick it only if that extra excitement is worth it to you, with an entertainment budget.

Does the Joker have a rollover like La Primitiva?

No. The Joker pays fixed prizes with no rollover: the top prize is always €1,000,000, whoever wins and whatever the takings. If in a given week nobody matches all seven digits, that million doesn't carry over to the next one, unlike La Primitiva's jackpot, which does grow when it goes unclaimed. So if what you're after is dreaming of very big prizes during rollover weeks, that thrill comes from La Primitiva, not the Joker.

Is there a number to win the lottery today in 2026?

There isn't one — neither a number nor a method, not in the Joker either. In fact, the Joker number is assigned by the system at random, precisely so that nobody can 'pick the winning one'. There is no figure, system or 'code' that guarantees a prize; if there were, nobody would be selling it. Every seven-digit number has exactly the same probability. The sensible thing is to play for fun, with your head, and with an entertainment budget.

How does the '1220 code' work for winning?

It doesn't. The '1220 code' is numerology that does the rounds on social media promising to predict results, and it's just one more of those supposedly foolproof methods. It's no use for either La Primitiva or the Joker: the Joker number is generated at random and each draw is independent, so there's no pattern to calculate and no code that can guess it. The only real thing about the Joker is its odds, which are published and the same for everyone.
