Spain's record: €190 million (2017)

 

The biggest EuroMillions prize paid out in Spain is €190 million, from the draw of 6 October 2017. The ticket, held by a single winner, was validated in Las Palmas de Gran Canaria.

At the time, €190 million was the maximum cap the game could pay out, so that prize couldn't have been any bigger. It remains, to this day, the largest amount awarded to a single ticket in our country.

The biggest jackpot ever: €250 million

 

The EuroMillions ceiling has risen over the years to the current €250 million, a figure that was reached for the first time in 2025. That year the jackpot hit the maximum and was paid out, with winning tickets outside Spain.

Before reaching that cap, the biggest jackpot ever awarded in the entire history of the draw had been €240 million, in December 2023. Since these records are updated over time, it's best to check the current figure on the official Loterías and EuroMillions website.

Why does the jackpot have a cap?

 

The EuroMillions jackpot doesn't grow without limit: it has a maximum (today, €250 million). Once it reaches that, it holds at that figure for a limited number of draws.

If no one matches the top tier within that window, the first-prize amount doesn't disappear: it rolls down to be shared among the winners of the next tier that has winners. The specific details of the rules are worth checking in the official source, because it's the rules of the game that set how many draws the cap holds for.

The odds, in perspective

Frequently asked questions about the biggest EuroMillions jackpots

 
What is the biggest EuroMillions jackpot in Spain?

€190 million, from the draw of 6 October 2017. The ticket, held by a single winner, was validated in Las Palmas de Gran Canaria.

And the biggest jackpot ever?

€250 million, the game's maximum cap, reached for the first time in 2025. Before that ceiling, the biggest ever paid was €240 million (December 2023).

How high can the jackpot go?

Up to its maximum cap, today €250 million. Once it gets there, the jackpot holds at that figure for a limited number of draws and, if there's no top-tier winner, the prize rolls down to the next tier.

Why is there a limit?

It's a rule of the game: it stops the jackpot from growing endlessly. The rules set the cap and how many draws it can hold for before rolling down. It's worth checking the details in the official source.

Are the odds better if the jackpot is very big?

No. The odds of winning are the same whatever the jackpot. A big jackpot attracts more people, but it doesn't change the chances of your bet.

Where do I check the current figures?

On the official Loterías y Apuestas del Estado website and the EuroMillions one. Records are updated with every big jackpot, so the valid reference is always the official source.
