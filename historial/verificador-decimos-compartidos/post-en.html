Sharing a décimo (lottery ticket) at the office is all excitement; proving whose it was once there's a prize is another matter. A décimo is a bearer instrument: whoever holds it in their hand can cash it in. If the split is left at a vague 'we trust each other' or a stray message, proving that the prize belonged to several people can turn into a serious dispute.

The answer isn't to distrust anyone — it's to put it in writing. Why a signed document protects you more than a WhatsApp message

 

As long as there's no prize, nobody argues; the problem appears the moment one comes in. A verbal agreement is valid, but you have to be able to prove it, and that's where most informal splits fall apart. A WhatsApp message helps, but on its own it's weak evidence: a photo of the décimo doesn't say who's taking part or for how much, and a stray 'ok' can always be disputed.

A contract signed by everyone, dated before the draw, makes clear who's playing, with what share, and who keeps the décimo in safekeeping. It isn't red tape: it's the simplest way to make sure the prize reaches, in full, whoever it's owed to, and to stop a refusal to share from turning into a legal problem for misappropriation.

What the contract template includes

 

The template brings together everything a properly done split needs, in a single document ready to sign:

→ Identification of the décimo: number, series, fraction, draw, date, lottery office and amount.

→ Participants and shares: a table with each person's name, DNI, contribution in euros and percentage, adding up to 100%.

→ Safekeeping: who keeps the décimo and under what obligations, making clear that safekeeping doesn't change the shares.

→ Claiming and sharing: how the prize is claimed jointly and shared in proportion to each person's part.

→ Joint ownership before the Spanish tax authority (Hacienda): that everyone is recorded as joint owners of the prize, so that each person is taxed on their own share and the split isn't treated as a gift.

→ Signatures from everyone: space for each participant's name, DNI and signature.

The safety verifier: 7 checks before the draw

 

Before you play, run through this list. If you can tick all seven, your syndicate is protected:

✓The décimo is identified (number, series, fraction and draw), with no ambiguity.

✓All the participants are listed, with their part in euros or percentage, and the total comes to 100%.

✓The agreement is dated before the draw; nothing is decided after seeing whether there's a prize.

✓It's clear who keeps the décimo in safekeeping and that they can show it to anyone who asks.

✓There's a record of each person's payment (a Bizum or a transfer with a reference).

✓Everyone has signed and each participant keeps a copy.

✓You know that, if the prize is large, you'll declare the joint ownership when you claim it.

Frequently asked questions about the contract template for syndicates

 
Is the contract template free?

Yes. The private contract template for syndicates is free and provided by Lotería Las Arenas. You just have to download the PDF, print it out, fill in the details of the décimo and of each participant, and all sign it before the draw. It's a template for general guidance, designed so that you can put the split in writing without any hassle.

Is a private contract between private individuals legally valid?

Yes. In Spain, a private agreement signed between private individuals is valid and binding on the parties; there's no need to go to a notary to split a décimo. The template is drawn up on the basis of the Civil Code (freedom of contract and co-ownership of property). For high-value prizes or complex cases, it's also worth getting professional advice, but for an office syndicate this signed document is very solid protection.

What details of the décimo do you need to put down?

The ones that identify the décimo beyond any doubt: number, series, fraction, draw and date, as well as the lottery office where it was bought and the amount. The more complete the identification, the easier it is to prove, should it come to it, exactly which décimo you were sharing and in which draw.

How is the prize shared if it wins?

In proportion to the shares each participant has set out in the contract. The template provides for the prize to be claimed jointly and, when the amount requires identifying yourself to the bank, for everyone to be recorded as joint owners of the prize. That way each person is taxed on their own part and it stops the Spanish tax authority (Hacienda) from treating the split as a gift from whoever claims it to the rest.

Does everyone need to sign it?

Yes, it's the recommended thing to do: the agreement belongs to everyone, so each participant should sign and, if possible, keep a copy. A document signed by only one person doesn't reflect the common agreement. Back it up too with a traceable payment for each part (a Bizum or transfer with a clear reference), which leaves a record of who contributed and how much.

Does this replace advice from a solicitor?

No. The template is a template for general guidance to help you put the split in writing; it isn't tailored legal or tax advice. For very large prizes or particular situations (inheritances, minors, non-residents, etc.), the sensible thing is to consult a solicitor or adviser. For most office syndicates, on the other hand, signing this contract before the draw avoids practically all disputes.
