What time is the Thursday National Lottery draw?

 

The Thursday draw takes place at 21:00 (mainland Spanish time), every week. Beforehand, at 20:30, sales for that draw close for good, so if you want to play a specific number it's best not to leave it until the last minute. These are the key times:

→ 20:30 — Sales close: from that time onwards you can no longer buy tickets for that night's draw.

→ 21:00 — Live draw: it begins punctually from the SELAE Draw Hall, using the system of multiple drums.

→ Around 9:15–9:30 pm — Official Prize List: shortly afterwards the list is published with all the prizes, approximations (aproximaciones), hundreds (centenas), last-two-digit prizes (terminaciones) and refunds (reintegro).

Bear in mind that this is mainland Spanish time: in the Canary Islands the draw is followed an hour earlier according to local time. And don't confuse it with the Saturday draw, which is a different draw altogether (it costs twice as much and pays out twice as much).

The weekly draw ritual

 

For many people, the Thursday draw is an almost ceremonial routine: at nine o'clock sharp, the live broadcast on and the ticket to hand. It isn't the great Christmas event, but it has its own liturgy, discreet and steady, repeated week after week throughout the year.

That steadiness is, precisely, part of its charm. While the big special draws come once or twice a year, the Thursday one keeps hope alive on a regular basis, with a cheap ticket and a prize that, without turning your life upside down, is very welcome. That's why it has such a loyal following: the people who never miss their number, their lottery office and their nine o'clock appointment.

Where to watch the real live broadcast (and why to be wary of copies)

 

The draw's official signal is provided by the Spanish State Lottery and RTVE. You can follow it on SELAE's official website and app, on its official channel and via RTVE. Those are the reliable sources; any other broadcast is, at the very least, unofficial.

Why does it matter so much? Because social media is full of re-uploaded live streams, recordings of draws from other days or delayed videos that can lead to confusion. A 'live stream' that isn't the official one may show you a wrong result or one from another date, and a single misread figure is enough to make you believe you've won —or that you haven't— when the opposite is true.

The rule is simple: to watch the draw, official sources; to check whether you've won a prize, the Official Prize List. What is called out live is the show; what pays out is the official list published by SELAE once it's over.

How to check your tickets precisely

 

Checking a ticket properly is more than looking at the first prize: you have to review the complete number and all the associated categories. You have three reliable ways to do it, and all of them rely on the official result:

→ Official online checker: you enter your number into the Loterías checker and it tells you instantly whether it has won a prize and in which category.

→ Scanning the ticket's QR code: using your phone's camera or the official app, you read the ticket's code and clear up any doubts without typing anything.

→ At your lottery office: the terminal checks the ticket and confirms your prize, which you can collect there depending on the amount.

Whatever you do, always wait for the Official Prize List: don't rely on what you thought you heard in the live broadcast, or on an unconfirmed result. That small habit is the difference between collecting everything you're owed and leaving a small prize unclaimed.

Frequently asked questions about the Thursday draw

 
What time is the Thursday National Lottery draw?

At 21:00 (mainland Spanish time), every week. Sales for that draw close beforehand, at 20:30, so it's best to buy with time to spare. The draw takes place in the Draw Hall of the Spanish State Lottery (Loterías y Apuestas del Estado) using the system of multiple drums, and the Official Prize List is published shortly afterwards, around 9:15–9:30 pm.

Where can you watch the Thursday draw live?

On the official sources: Loterías y Apuestas del Estado (its website, app and official channel) and RTVE. Those are the reliable broadcasts. Be wary of 'live streams' doing the rounds on social media, because they may be recordings from other days or delayed videos. To watch the draw, official sources; to check prizes, the Official Prize List.

How do I know if a draw live stream is the official one?

Because it comes from SELAE or RTVE, not from just any account or website. An unofficial live stream may show a draw from another date or run with a delay, and that leads to mistakes when checking. The advice is clear: follow the draw on the official channels and, to find out whether your ticket has won a prize, don't rely on what you heard, but on the Official Prize List published by Loterías.

How much does a Thursday ticket cost and how much does the first prize pay out?

The Thursday ticket costs €3, and the first prize pays out €30,000 per ticket; the second, €6,000 per ticket. There are also approximations, hundreds, last-two-digit prizes and a refund, which pay out even if your number isn't the first. The Saturday draw is different: it costs twice as much (the ticket is more expensive) and pays out twice as much in every category.

How do I check whether my Thursday ticket has won a prize?

You have three reliable ways: the official online checker (you enter your number), scanning the QR code on the ticket with your phone or the official app, or going to your lottery office, where the terminal checks it. In all three cases, the result is matched against the Official Prize List. Review the complete number and all the categories, not just the first prize.

Do the Thursday draw prizes pay tax?

Lottery prizes are only taxed above €40,000. As those from the Thursday draw fall below that figure (the first prize is €30,000 per ticket), they are collected in full, with no withholding. Even so, for amounts and tax changes it's always best to check the current regulations or ask us at the lottery office.
