How to organise the Christmas Lottery at your company in 2026

Organising the Christmas Lottery at your company starts with three decisions: what you are going to buy, who will pay for each purchase and how each participant will receive their tickets (décimos). Once these points are clear, it becomes far easier to request the reservation, announce the campaign and coordinate delivery.

At Lotería Las Arenas we put together campaigns tailored to companies and organisations. We can set up a private online space for employees and agree on the purchase options that suit your team. You don't need to use every option: it's best to choose the ones you will genuinely need. If you like, start with our Christmas lottery for companies service and we'll adapt it to your case.

In brief

Three decisions and one golden rule: never mix the options

Before you announce anything, make clear who buys (each employee, or the company as a gift), who pays and how tickets are delivered (deposit and safekeeping, home delivery, joint delivery at the company, in-person purchase or direct sale at your premises). Taking part must be voluntary, your campaign's closing date is not necessarily the day of the draw, and each option has its own tasks: choose the ones you are going to use and don't mix them.

Start by defining who buys and who pays

 

A campaign in which each employee buys their own lottery is not the same as a gift funded by the company. In the first case, the company simply makes buying easier; in the second, it needs to pin down an order and its budget. You can also offer both, but it's worth keeping them separate from the start.

Appoint a point of contact to coordinate the campaign with the lottery office. They don't have to collect any money: their role can be limited to agreeing the terms, handing out the instructions and sorting out internal organisational matters.

Taking part must be voluntary. Present it as an option for anyone who wants to join in, not as an obligation or as an opportunity no one can afford to miss.

Estimate the quantity before choosing the number

 

Use the actual purchases from last year's campaign as your reference, not just the tickets that were reserved. Also check whether the headcount has changed, how many sites will take part and whether the company will add an order to give as a gift.

When you have no track record, gauge the team's interest without turning that question into a purchase. An estimate lets you start the conversation; the quantity that ends up reserved will depend on availability and on the agreement with the lottery office.

You can request a specific number, specify an ending or ask for suggestions. At Las Arenas we check availability before confirming the provisional hold. Having sent us a preference does not mean the number is already reserved.

Choose the options (without mixing them)

 

Online purchases can be organised with deposit and safekeeping at our office, with home delivery or with joint delivery at the company, depending on what we agree for your campaign. We also offer in-person purchase at the office and reservations for direct sale at your company's premises. Here is a quick overview:

Deposit does not mean picking tickets up later. With this option the tickets are kept safe; you shouldn't tell the team that they will later be able to drop by and collect them as if they had chosen an in-person purchase. And if you take part through shares (participaciones) rather than whole tickets, say so from the start: it requires a different set-up, and it isn't enough simply to change the amount in the announcement.

Agree the dates before announcing the campaign

 

SELAE sets the Christmas Draw for 22 December, but that date should not be confused with your company's purchase deadline. The purchase window, how long the reservation stays valid and the delivery all need to be agreed for the campaign.

Don't copy last year's closing date or another company's. If there are several options, check whether they have different dates. Also make clear who to turn to with a query and what to do when someone asks to buy after the deadline: the answer shouldn't promise an extension that hasn't yet been confirmed.

Communicate only what is already in place

 

A useful message covers the number and draw, the access provided for your company, the options available, the relevant deadline and a contact for help. At Las Arenas we prepare the platform, the password, the poster and the instructions to match the agreed option.

Don't reuse a password or a link from a previous campaign without checking them first. If the notice goes out through several internal channels, keep the content identical so that contradictory versions don't start circulating.

Before the deadline, an informative reminder can help the team check the date and the options. Avoid messages that pressure people into buying or spending more.

Close the campaign according to the option you chose

 

For a grouped delivery, coordinate receipt and distribution. For a purchase with safekeeping, don't arrange a collection that isn't part of that option. And if you have reserved lottery for direct sale, check the agreed terms without assuming that unsold tickets can be returned.

Make a note of what worked: the quantity actually bought, common queries and coordination difficulties. That information will help you plan next year on a realistic footing.

Frequently asked questions about the company Christmas Lottery

 
Who pays for the company Christmas Lottery, the company or each employee?

It can be either, and it's worth deciding at the start. In an individual campaign, each employee buys their own lottery and the company simply provides access. In a company gift, the business funds an order from its own budget. You can also combine the two, but keep them separate from the outset, because the organisation and the communication are different in each case.

Do you need to collect money from employees?

Not necessarily. The point of contact who coordinates the campaign doesn't have to collect any money: their role can be limited to agreeing the terms with the lottery office, handing out the instructions and answering internal queries. What's more, buying online can avoid collecting money, although it doesn't automatically remove other tasks, such as deciding who receives the shipment or how it is distributed internally.

What purchase and delivery options do you offer companies?

We work with several, and it's best not to mix them: online purchase with deposit and safekeeping at the office, home delivery, joint delivery at the company (with the list of buyers), in-person purchase and a reservation for direct sale on your premises. Each option has its own tasks and dates; together we choose the one that best fits your campaign.

Does reserving a number guarantee that it's set aside for the company?

Sending a preference does not mean the number is already reserved. You can ask for a specific number, specify an ending or request suggestions, but at Las Arenas we always check availability before confirming the provisional hold. Until that confirmation, the reservation isn't finalised.

Is the campaign's closing date 22 December?

Not necessarily. SELAE sets the Christmas Draw for 22 December, but that date should not be confused with your company's purchase deadline, which is agreed for each campaign. The purchase window, how long the reservation stays valid and the delivery are set to suit your needs, and if there are several options they may have different dates.

Can you play with shares (participaciones) instead of whole tickets?

Yes, but you need to say so at the start. Playing with shares (participaciones) rather than whole tickets requires a different set-up (amounts, distribution and paperwork); it isn't enough simply to change the amount in the announcement. If that's your case, tell us when you set up the campaign and we'll prepare it accordingly.
