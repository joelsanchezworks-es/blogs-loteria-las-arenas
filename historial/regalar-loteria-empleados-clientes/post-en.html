Giving the Christmas Lottery as a gift to employees and clients

Banner · Christmas Lottery tickets prepared as a company gift for employees and clients

Giving the Christmas Lottery as a gift to employees or clients is different from organising a campaign for each person to buy on their own. With a gift, the company bears the cost and must specify what it hands over, to whom and on what terms.

Before choosing the number or preparing the message, define the budget and the number of recipients. Then check the availability of the décimos (lottery tickets) and how to organise the order with Lotería Las Arenas, through our company service.

The key point

The gift is the share, not a promise of returns

With a gift the company pays and decides what it hands over, to whom and how. It is best to keep it separate from voluntary purchases (whatever each person chooses to buy on their own), to choose the number without promising 'more luck', and to agree on purchase and delivery before giving dates. The gift is the share in the draw, not a recoverable investment.

Define the gift and its budget

 

Decide whether you will hand over whole décimos or need to look into shares. They are not equivalent formats: they change the amount each person plays and the organisation needed to identify their share.

The Christmas lottery ticket costs 20 €, according to SELAE. As a budget example, giving one décimo to 60 people as a gift means 1.200 € in lottery. This calculation does not include other costs that may apply to the order or the delivery.

Do not confuse that amount with a recoverable investment. The gift is the share in the draw, not a promise of financial return.

Keep the gift and voluntary purchases separate

 

If the company hands over a décimo and also makes it easy for the employee to buy more, explain the two things separately. The recipient must know what they receive without paying and what they buy, if they decide to, with their own budget.

Do not make the additional purchase seem necessary in order to receive the gift. Nor is it wise to mix both channels in a single list that makes it impossible to tell the company's order apart from individual purchases.

When it comes to clients, also think about how receipt will be coordinated and about the gift policies that may apply at each organisation. The aim is for the delivery to be clear, not to create a commercial obligation.

Choose the number without promising a prize advantage

 

You can request a number that makes sense for the company or ask for available suggestions. Availability must be checked for the quantity you need; it is not enough for the number to appear in material from another campaign.

A date or an ending can help personalise the message, but they must not be presented as a way to increase the chances of a prize.

In the Las Arenas identity we use the expression 'Tickets blessed by the Golden Stone (Piedra de Oro)'. It is part of our brand communication: a charming wink, not a guarantee of a prize, and it does not change how the draw works.

Agree on how it will be bought and delivered

 

When you get in touch, state that this is an order funded by the company and explain whether you need a joint delivery or some other arrangement. Confirm the terms of the order, the payment and the delivery before promising a date to the recipients.

Do not assume that the individual employee purchase space is enough on its own to handle any corporate gift. The arrangement must match the actual operation.

If safekeeping is being considered, the purchase and its ownership must be set out explicitly. Buying online with a deposit does not mean that those décimos can later be collected to hand over as a gift.

Identify what is handed over

 

As an organisational recommendation, keep sufficient record of the recipient and of the décimo or share they receive. If one person coordinates several deliveries, that control helps avoid duplication and later confusion.

When shares are handed over, the number, the draw, the amount played and the applicable instructions must be clear. Do not advertise a whole décimo when what the person receives is a part of one.

If the format raises questions about ownership or proof, resolve them before the draw with the relevant adviser. A simple generic line of congratulations is no substitute for identifying the gift.

Review the tax and accounting side of your case

 

The purchase, the handing over of a gift and any prize that may be won are separate matters. This guide does not grant the company an automatic tax deduction, nor does it assume that all gifts to employees and clients are treated the same way.

Before placing the order, check with your advisers how to document it and what obligations apply to your situation. If the party that wins a prize is a company, the treatment for an individual should not simply be applied: the Spanish Tax Agency (Agencia Tributaria) distinguishes between the two cases.

General information, not advice. Giving lottery as a gift to employees or clients can have tax and accounting implications that depend on each case (benefit in kind, expenses, company prizes…). This guide is no substitute for your advisers' judgement: check with a professional how to document the gift and what obligations apply to you before placing the order.

Frequently asked questions about giving lottery as a gift to employees and clients

 
How much does it cost to give a décimo to the workforce as a gift?

The Christmas lottery ticket costs 20 €, according to SELAE. As a rough example, giving one décimo to 60 people comes to 1.200 € in lottery. That calculation does not include other costs that may apply to the order or the delivery, and it is best not to confuse the amount with a recoverable investment: the gift is the share in the draw, not a promise of returns.

Is giving a décimo as a gift the same as organising a voluntary purchase?

No. With a gift, the company bears the cost and decides what it hands over; with a voluntary purchase, each employee buys on their own and with their own budget. It is best to explain them separately so that the recipient knows what they receive without paying and what they would buy if they wish. The two channels should not be mixed in a single list, nor should the additional purchase be made to seem necessary in order to receive the gift.

Can I choose a special number for the gift?

Yes: you can request a number that makes sense for the company (a date, an ending) or ask for available suggestions, always checking availability for the quantity you need. That said: a special number serves to personalise the message, not to promise more luck. All numbers have the same probability, and brand expressions such as 'Tickets blessed by the Golden Stone (Piedra de Oro)' are a wink, not a guarantee of a prize.

If we buy with a deposit, can we later collect the décimos to give them as gifts?

It is best not to take it for granted. Buying online with a deposit leaves the décimos in safekeeping, and that arrangement does not mean they can later be collected to hand over as a gift. If you are going to distribute the gift physically, the purchase, its ownership and a suitable delivery arrangement must be set out explicitly: the individual employee purchase space is not enough on its own for any corporate gift.
