Gifting lottery at Christmas: why it's a tradition in Spain

In Spain, gifting a décimo (a tenth-share of a ticket) is almost a language of its own: a way of saying 'thinking of you' and 'may we win together'. We'll tell you where that custom comes from and why, deep down, what you're giving isn't money: it's hope.

Gifting lottery at Christmas is a tradition in Spain because it's the simplest way to share hope: a small gesture, within anyone's reach, that says 'I want this to happen to both of us'. You don't give it for the prize —which almost never comes— but for the thrill of waiting for it together.

Behind it lie more than two centuries of custom. We'll tell you where it comes from, what it really means and why we keep doing it every December.

In brief

Why we gift lottery

We gift lottery because it means sharing hope, not money: a décimo (or a share) is a gift that's universal, inexpensive and full of intention. The Christmas Lottery Draw has been held since 1812, and with it grew the custom of handing out and gifting décimos among family, friends and colleagues. What matters isn't winning: it's waiting together.

Why do we gift lottery at Christmas?

 

Because it's a gift everyone understands. You don't have to get the size or the color right: a décimo works for your brother-in-law, for the neighbor, for the kids' teacher. And it carries a lovely message inside: 'I'd love for you to win' or, better still, 'may we both win'.

It's also a democratic gift: it costs little and anyone can give it. That's why it slips into company dinners, Secret Santa exchanges and family meals. If you'd like to give it a more special twist, the Christmas superdécimo to gift is a lovely way to do it.

The origin of the tradition

 

The Christmas Lottery Draw has been held in Spain since 1812, and it's one of the oldest in the world. Over the years it stopped being just a draw and became a collective ritual: 22 December kicking off the holidays, the children of San Ildefonso (who sing the numbers), and the décimos that pass from hand to hand.

Gifting and sharing lottery grew within that tradition. If you enjoy these stories, we tell the full story in the history, traditions and trivia of the Christmas Lottery.

Sharing the hope: the real gift

 

The lovely thing about gifting lottery is that the prize is the least of it. What you're really giving is the week of hope, the 'what if we win?' conversation, the photo of the décimo in the family group chat. That always arrives, whether you win or not.

That gesture repeats everywhere: the grandfather who splits a décimo among the grandchildren, the group of friends who have played the same number for years, the company that treats its team. In lottery offices like ours, in the C.C. Arenas de Barcelona, it's one of the most heartwarming sights we see every December.

Myths and trivia (and an uncomfortable truth)

 

The tradition brings its own mythology: 'lucky' numbers, endings that 'come up more often', lottery offices 'that hand out more prizes'. It's all great for the excitement, but it's worth remembering one simple truth: every number has exactly the same probability.

Don't let that take away the magic — quite the opposite: pick the number that means something to you and enjoy it. If you enjoy these stories, you'll find more in the myths and legends of the Christmas Lottery.

Gifting well: a note to avoid mix-ups

A practical detail: when you give a décimo, make it clear that it's a gift and who it now belongs to. The lottery is a bearer instrument (al portador), so a timely 'this is yours' avoids misunderstandings if it happens to win. With that sorted, enjoy the tradition with no fine print.

Frequently asked questions about the tradition of gifting lottery at Christmas

 
Why is lottery gifted at Christmas in Spain?

Because it's the simplest way to share hope: a décimo is a universal, inexpensive gift with a lovely message behind it ('may we win together'). It's a custom tied to the Christmas Lottery Draw, which has been held since 1812.

How long has the Christmas Lottery Draw existed?

Since 1812. It's one of the oldest draws in the world, and over the years it became a collective ritual: 22 December, the children of San Ildefonso (who sing the numbers) and the décimos that are shared out and given as gifts.

Is it better to give a décimo, a share or a superdécimo?

It depends on the gesture you want to make. A share (participaciones) is ideal for sharing with lots of people for very little; the décimo is the classic gift; and the superdécimo is a version designed especially for gifting. Any of them does its job: sharing hope.

Does gifting lottery bring good luck, or is it just tradition?

It's tradition and affection, not luck: every number has the same probability. What's valuable about the gesture is the shared hope, not any real advantage in winning. Give it for what it means, not for what it 'might' win.

If I give a décimo and it wins, whose is the prize?

The lottery is a bearer instrument (al portador): in principle, it belongs to whoever holds the décimo. That's why, when you give it, it's best to make clear that it's a gift and who it now belongs to, to avoid misunderstandings.
