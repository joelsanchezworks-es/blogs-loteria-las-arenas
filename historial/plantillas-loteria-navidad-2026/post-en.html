Before you share anything

A WhatsApp photo is not a share slip

Sending the image of a décimo to a group doesn’t split the ownership or prove anything: it doesn’t say who’s taking part, with how much, or who is holding the number. For sharing a décimo to be safe, you need a share-slip document with some minimum details. That’s what we give you here.

Why people share the Christmas Lottery (and why you should do it properly)

 

Sharing is part of the magic of Christmas: it multiplies the excitement, lets you play more numbers for less money and strengthens the bonds of the syndicate, the family or your workmates. A décimo costs €20, and splitting it into share slips of €5 or €10 lets everyone buy into the dream without anyone feeling the pinch.

The problem isn’t sharing, but sharing without leaving any record. As long as it doesn’t win, it makes no difference: it’s a game, a collective bit of excitement. But the day that number comes up a winner, it suddenly matters enormously who put in what, who’s keeping the décimo and how it gets split. And if all you have is a photo in a chat and everyone’s memory, the party can quickly sour.

Two fronts come into play here. The first is the family one: without a clear split in writing, misunderstandings creep in (‘I put in more’, ‘that’s not what we agreed’). The second is the tax one: if the prize is large and there’s no record of who is a co-owner, Hacienda can treat the split as a gift (for tax purposes) and make you pay more than you should. Both are avoided by the same thing: a properly made share slip.

Tips for using the template

 

Having the template is only half the job; using it well is the other half. With these steps, sharing your décimo will be as safe as it is exciting:

1

Fill it in before the draw, not after

The joint ownership has to be clear from the outset. Documenting who took part once the prize is known is exactly what can be interpreted as a gift.

2

One copy for each participant

Each person should keep their own share slip, with their amount clearly noted. Not a single sheet that one person keeps and everyone else simply takes on trust.

3

Leave a money trail

If someone fronts the payment, have the others send them a Bizum or bank transfer with the reference ‘shared décimo Christmas 2026’. That evidence backs up the share slip.

4

Clearly identify the custodian

Make sure everyone knows who keeps the original décimo and where. If it’s held at a lottery office, all the better: it can’t get lost, damaged or ‘turn up’ in the wrong pocket.

5

Check the number and keep everything together

Check that the number on the template matches the one on the décimo, and keep the share slip with the proof of payment until after the draw and the payout.

Five minutes of paperwork today saves you an enormous headache on 22 December. And if you’d rather not deal with any of this, that’s what we lottery offices are here for.

Frequently asked questions

 
Does a WhatsApp photo of the décimo work as a share slip?

On its own, no. A photo proves which number exists, but it doesn’t prove who takes part, with how much, or who is holding the décimo, and anyone can have the same image. For sharing to be safe you need a share slip with three minimum details: the décimo identified (number, draw and date), who takes part and with what amount, and the custodian plus the proof of payment. With those three requirements —ideally with a lottery-office stamp— your share is protected; without them, faced with a big prize it may be worth nothing at all.

How do you share a Christmas décimo without problems with Hacienda?

By documenting joint ownership before the draw. That is, putting in writing who takes part and with what amount from the start, not once it’s known to have won. The first €40,000 per décimo is tax-exempt (shared among the co-owners according to their percentage) and the rest is taxed at 20%. If the prize is collected at the bank, ideally all the co-owners should go along, or whoever goes should take a document with the names and percentages, so that the withholding is applied to each person. That way you avoid a later split being interpreted as a gift.

What should a share-slip template include?

At a minimum: the draw and the date (Christmas Lottery, 22/12/2026), the number of the décimo, the total price of the décimo, the name of each participant and the amount they’re putting in, who the custodian is (who keeps the décimo) and a signature or stamp to validate it. Print one copy for each participant and keep it together with the proof of payment. The more verifiable details, the stronger the share slip.

What is digital custody and how does it prevent disputes?

It’s a service through which the lottery office keeps the décimo and records who takes part and with what amount from the moment it’s bought. It’s the way to avoid both family fights and tax scares, because it eliminates the ‘I put in more’ and the ‘who was keeping the décimo?’ at the root.
