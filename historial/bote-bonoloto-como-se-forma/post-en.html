The jackpot is what makes some Bonoloto draws grab more attention: when nobody wins, the top prize rolls over and grows. But it is worth understanding exactly how it works so you do not get carried away by the thrill of the 'record jackpot'.

Bonoloto is drawn Monday to Saturday, you choose 6 numbers from 1 to 49 and each bet costs €0.50. Everything comes from there: the prizes in each category and, where applicable, the jackpot. Let's go through it step by step.

An honest spoiler: a bigger jackpot means a bigger prize if you win, but it does not improve your odds of getting it.

In brief

The jackpot rolls over; the probability does not

The jackpot is the first-category prize (matching the 6 numbers) when there are no winners: that money rolls over to the next draw and so it keeps growing. It is formed from a share of the total stakes. The important thing: the probability of matching the 6 is always the same (1 in 13,983,816), whether the jackpot is big or small.

What the Bonoloto jackpot is

 

The jackpot is, simply, the accumulated first-category prize. In Bonoloto, the first category is won by matching the 6 numbers in the draw. When nobody manages it in a draw, that prize does not disappear: it is kept and added to the one for the next occasion.

That is why the jackpot rises and falls: if someone matches the 6, the jackpot 'is shared out' and starts again; if not, it keeps swelling draw after draw. It is not a separate prize, but the first-category pot that keeps accumulating.

How the jackpot forms

 

Every €0.50 bet contributes to a common prize fund. A part of what is taken in each draw goes towards paying the prizes in the various categories; the portion corresponding to the first category is the one that forms the jackpot.

If that first category is left with no winners, its allocation passes in full to the next draw. So a high jackpot does not come out of nowhere: it is the sum of several draws in a row with no winner of the 6 numbers.

What happens if nobody matches the 6 numbers

 

This is the key question, and the answer is simple: the jackpot rolls over. If nobody matches the first category in a draw, the prize reserved for it is moved to the next draw, which then starts with a bigger jackpot.

That process can repeat for several draws in a row, and it is precisely what creates the eye-catching jackpots. As long as nobody matches the 6, the jackpot keeps growing; as soon as someone does, it is shared out and the cycle starts again from zero.

Is it worth playing when the jackpot grows?

 

Here is the honest part: a bigger jackpot does not improve your odds. The chance of matching the 6 numbers is always the same, 1 in 13,983,816, whether the jackpot is modest or a record one. The only thing that changes is the size of the prize if you win, not the chances of winning.

So the answer is personal: if the idea of playing for a high jackpot excites you, go ahead, but use your head. Play responsibly, set aside only what you want for the fun of it and do not fall into playing too much 'because there is a jackpot'. The jackpot is a lure, not a statistical advantage.
