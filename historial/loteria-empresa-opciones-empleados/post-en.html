When it comes to choosing how to buy the company’s Christmas Lottery, there is a more useful question than ‘online or in person?’: where do you want your ticket (décimo) to end up after you buy it? Buying online and keeping it in safekeeping is not the same as buying online to receive it at home or as part of a joint delivery.

At Lotería Las Arenas we agree the options for each company’s campaign together. Your staff should follow the instructions in their own space, because not every option has to be available at the same time. If you like, we can help you pin them down through our lottery for companies service.

In brief

The key is not ‘online or in person’ but where the ticket ends up

There are five options and it helps not to confuse them: deposit and safekeeping (the ticket is kept at the lottery office), home delivery, joint delivery at the company, in-person purchase and direct sale at your premises. The most misunderstood is safekeeping: buying online with safekeeping is not the same as reserving to collect later. Choose the option according to your team’s presence, the need for physical delivery and who can coordinate the handout.

Online purchase with deposit and safekeeping

 

Your employee buys online and the tickets are held in safekeeping at our lottery office. This is an option for anyone who does not need to receive the physical document as part of that purchase.

The essential difference is that it is not the same as reserving online to collect later. Nor does it include a later delivery or a handout at the office by default: a purchase with physical delivery must be made through the option enabled for it.

When you organise the campaign, explain this difference before your staff buy. It will stop anyone from choosing safekeeping while thinking they have requested a delivery.

Online purchase with home delivery

 

This option lets anyone who has it available ask to receive their tickets at the delivery address given for their order.

Before confirming the purchase, you should check the applicable terms, the accepted destination and the delivery cost. Do not take it for granted that delivery is free, or that a rate or a deadline from another campaign still applies.

It can suit an employee who does not usually come into the office. For a company with several sites, it helps to communicate clearly which options have been enabled and to avoid promising universal coverage.

Online purchase with joint delivery at the company

 

When this option is agreed, your staff buy and the delivery is coordinated jointly for the company, with a list of buyers.

The organisational advantage is that it separates the individual purchase from the grouped receipt. There must, however, be someone responsible for receiving the tickets and coordinating their internal distribution in line with the list.

That is why it is not right to present this option as ‘the company does nothing’. It may save one person from collecting payment for the purchases, but it keeps a physical coordination task.

In-person purchase at our lottery office

 

The purchase is made at Lotería Las Arenas. Your employee should follow the identification and reservation instructions communicated for their campaign, without assuming that simply naming any company is enough.

This option does not make tickets already bought with online deposit collectable: they are different options. It is also worth checking the current opening hours before travelling.

Reservation for direct sale at your premises

 

For certain campaigns, a reservation can be agreed for direct sale at the company’s premises. Before announcing it, you need to pin down how it will be organised, who will be involved and what dates and terms it will have.

It should not be confused with the delivery of tickets already bought online. In a sale the purchase is made; in a joint delivery, purchases made beforehand are handed out.

What if the company buys to give as a gift?

 

This is a different approach: the company funds an order for its recipients. It is best to spell this out from the start, rather than telling each employee to buy on their own.

The same goes for shares (participaciones): they are not a synonym for a ticket. Check how they are specifically organised before preparing announcements or handing out receipts.

Frequently asked questions about the options for employees

 
What is the difference between deposit with safekeeping and home delivery?

With deposit and safekeeping, your employee buys online and the ticket is kept at our lottery office; the physical document is not received as part of that purchase. With home delivery, the ticket is sent to the address given for the order, after first checking the destination, cost and terms. They are different options: one keeps the ticket, the other delivers it.

If I buy online with deposit, can I then come and collect it?

No. An online purchase with deposit and safekeeping is not the same as reserving to collect later, and it does not include a delivery or a handout at the office by default. If what you want is to take the ticket away physically, you have to use the option enabled for it (home delivery, joint delivery or in-person purchase). It is best to explain this difference before buying to avoid any misunderstanding.

Is home delivery free?

It should not be taken for granted. Before confirming a purchase with delivery, it is worth checking the terms, the accepted destination and the cost that apply to your campaign. A rate or a deadline from a previous campaign will not necessarily still apply. That is why, for companies with several sites, we recommend communicating clearly which options have been enabled and not promising universal coverage.

What is joint delivery at the company?

It is an option in which your staff buy individually and the delivery is coordinated jointly for the company, together with a list of buyers. It separates the purchase from the grouped receipt, but it needs someone responsible for receiving the tickets and handing them out in line with the list. It does not mean that ‘the company does nothing’: it keeps a coordination task.

Is direct sale the same as joint delivery?

No. With direct sale at your premises, the purchase is made there; with a joint delivery, tickets bought beforehand are handed out. They are different things. Direct sale is agreed for certain campaigns and, before announcing it, you need to pin down how it is organised, who is involved and with what dates and terms.

Are shares the same as a ticket?

They are not a synonym. Shares (participaciones) have their own setup (amounts, distribution and receipts) and it is worth checking it before preparing announcements or handing out documents. If your campaign is going to run on shares rather than whole tickets, say so from the start and we will prepare it accordingly.
