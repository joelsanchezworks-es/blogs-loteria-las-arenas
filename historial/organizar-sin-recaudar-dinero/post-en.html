How to organise your company's Christmas Lottery without collecting money from employees

Ensuring that nobody in the office has to collect the lottery money is possible: all it takes is for each worker to make their own purchase. We explain how to separate the buying, the communication and the delivery so the organiser doesn't end up as the 'cashier'.

The way to avoid having one person in the company collect the Christmas Lottery money is to organise a campaign in which each worker makes their own purchase. Once individual online purchasing is agreed, the organiser doesn't need to personally take payment for those orders.

That doesn't mean all the management work disappears. The company still needs to coordinate the campaign and, if there is group delivery, receive and distribute the décimos (lottery tickets). The key is to separate the buying, the communication and the delivery. You can find out about our company lottery service.

The key

Separate the buying, the communication and the delivery

If each worker buys individually through the agreed channel, the organiser no longer collects payments. What doesn't disappear is the coordination: informing people, agreeing dates and, if there is group delivery, receiving and distributing. It's not about promising that 'nobody has to deal with anything', but about making sure each task has a clear owner.

The problem with mixing all the tasks together

 

In a campaign run through informal requests, one and the same person can end up noting down who's interested, taking in money and handing out décimos. And if several lists are circulating as well, it becomes hard to tell an intention to buy from a purchase actually made.

Individual purchasing makes a different set-up possible: each participant buys their décimos through the agreed channel and the company focuses its efforts on informing people and coordinating whatever falls to it.

It's not about promising a specific number of hours saved without measuring it. It's about identifying one task that can stop passing through the organiser's hands: collecting the payments for those individual purchases.

How it works with Lotería Las Arenas

 

First, you request the number or preferences and an approximate quantity. We check availability and confirm the relevant provisional hold. We then prepare the private virtual space, the password, the poster and the instructions according to the agreed option.

The company shares that information and each worker follows the intended purchase process. A reply to an internal survey does not replace the purchase; it's worth saying so clearly in the launch communication.

The closing date and the contact point for issues should also be agreed, so the organiser doesn't have to improvise answers for each employee.

Safekeeping also avoids physically handing out the décimos

 

In an online purchase with deposit, the décimos are held in safekeeping at our lottery office. They are not bought to be collected later or to arrive automatically at the office.

That option makes it possible to organise participation without physically handing out the décimos bought under safekeeping. But it should be chosen because it fits what the participant wants, not because it has been explained to them as a deferred collection.

Anyone who needs physical delivery should use an option enabled for that, with its own conditions.

Buying online and delivering at the company doesn't remove the distribution

 

When individual purchasing with group delivery is agreed, delivery to the company is coordinated using the list of buyers. The buying and the money collection don't have to go through the organiser, but there is still internal receiving and distribution.

Before launch, identify who will receive the décimos and how those not present on the handout day will be looked after. It's best not to leave that decision until delivery time.

Individual home delivery is another option where available. Its costs and conditions must be checked for the campaign and the order; they should not be presented as free by default.

What the company should carry on doing

 

The company should keep a point of contact, share the correct instructions and communicate the agreed dates. If there is group delivery, it also coordinates receiving and handing them out.

It should not promise that it knows about every purchase in real time through a dashboard if that feature has not been confirmed. For any list or tracking required, it's best to agree what information is available and how it will be used in the campaign.

Simplification works better when each task has an owner, not when it's announced that nobody has to deal with anything.

A simple change to the message to the team

 

Instead of asking workers to send the money to a colleague, the communication can state that the purchase is made individually through the campaign access, within the agreed deadline.

That message should be accompanied by the correct option: safekeeping, home delivery or group delivery, as appropriate. That way you don't swap a payment-collection problem for one about delivery expectations.

If the company is going to give lottery as a gift or organise shares, that needs to be set up separately. Not all campaigns follow the same individual-purchase route.

Frequently asked questions about organising the lottery without collecting money

 
How do I avoid having someone collect the lottery money?

By organising an individual-purchase campaign: each worker buys their décimos online through the campaign access, within the agreed deadline. That way the organiser doesn't need to take payment for those orders personally. What remains is the coordination (informing people, setting dates and, if there is group delivery, receiving and distributing), but the task of collecting payments stops passing through their hands.

Does individual purchasing eliminate all the organisational work?

No. It removes one specific task —collecting the payments— but the company still needs a point of contact, to share the correct instructions, to communicate the dates and, if there is group delivery, to coordinate receiving and distribution. Simplification works when each task has an owner, not when it's announced that nobody has to deal with anything.

If we buy with deposit, do employees collect the décimos afterwards?

No. With online deposit, the décimos are held in safekeeping at the lottery office: they are not bought to be collected later or to arrive automatically at the office. This option should be chosen because it fits what the participant wants, not explained as a 'deferred collection'. Anyone who needs the décimo on paper should use an enabled delivery option, with its conditions.

If they buy online but we deliver at the company, do we save ourselves the handout?

Not entirely. With group delivery, the buying and the money collection don't go through the organiser, but there is still an internal receiving and distribution. It's worth deciding before launch who will receive the décimos and how those not present on the handout day will be looked after, rather than leaving it to the last minute.

Will I be able to see in real time who has bought?

Only if that feature has been confirmed for your campaign. It's best not to promise a real-time tracking dashboard if it hasn't been agreed. For any list or tracking you need, the best thing is to agree at the outset what information will be available and how it will be used, avoiding taking for granted a tool that isn't part of what was agreed.

What changes in the message we send to the team?

Instead of asking people to send the money to a colleague, the notice states that the purchase is made individually through the campaign access and within the deadline. It should be accompanied by the correct option (safekeeping, home delivery or group delivery) so as not to swap a payment-collection problem for one about delivery expectations. And if there is a company gift or shares as well, those are set up separately.
