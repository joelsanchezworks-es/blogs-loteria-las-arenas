With 2 numbers you already have a prize. And with 1 number and 2 stars, you do too. Before discarding your ticket, check whether you fall into one of these tiers.

The most common reading error (before you write off your ticket)

 

And now we come to the part that could change your week. The most common reading error in EuroMillions isn't not knowing the numbers: it's not knowing how little it takes to win. Every week people look at their 5 numbers, see they've only matched a couple and bin the ticket thinking 'nothing'… when they actually had a prize.

Why does it happen so often? Because EuroMillions plays a bit of a trick on the eye. We're used to lotteries where you either match a fair few or there's nothing, and here it doesn't work like that: the game pays out across thirteen tiers, and the lower ones take very little. Because the jackpot is so enormous, your mind goes straight to 5+2 and dismisses everything else as if it didn't exist. But between 'the jackpot' and 'nothing' there are eleven intermediate tiers, and several are within reach of almost any ticket.

There are, specifically, three things that almost no one checks properly and that are worth looking at before you give up:

→ The stars: they aren't decorative numbers. They're what open up many tiers. With 1 number and 2 stars, there's already a prize; ignoring them is throwing money away.

→ The real minimum: with just 2 numbers matched (without a single star) you already have a prize. Yes, with two. A lot of people don't know this and discard winning tickets.

→ The El Millón code: it's a separate prize of €1,000,000, with its own code on the receipt. It doesn't depend on your numbers: always check it, because it's incredibly easy to overlook.

Put another way: don't read only the five big numbers. Before you write off your ticket, look at the stars, count whether you reach two matches and check the El Millón code. That ten-second review is exactly what separates the person who claims their prize from the one who bins it without realising.
