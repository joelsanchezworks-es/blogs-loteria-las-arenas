Choosing the number is almost as exciting as the draw itself. For many people it is a little ritual: looking for a special date, someone's birthday or that number they 'always' play. And with a superdécimo, that gesture gains even more charm.

Before we start, it is worth saying clearly and with no small print: no number is more likely than another. Choosing by dates or by a hunch is part of the fun, not a strategy for winning. That said, here are the most common criteria for choosing one you'll enjoy.

In brief

Choose with excitement, but keep your feet on the ground

You can choose your number by special dates, birthdays and anniversaries, numbers with a story for you or simply at random. All of these criteria are equally valid, because every number has the same probability. That said, the most sought-after ones (palindrome numbers, the current year, popular dates) sell out first, and the superdécimo is a limited edition: if you are after a particular one, don't wait.

Choosing with your heart: dates and numbers with meaning

 

The loveliest way to choose is the one that connects you with the number. It doesn't make it any more likely to win, but it does make it more yours, and that is precisely what turns a superdécimo into a gift or a keepsake. These are the most common criteria:

Criteria for choosing your number (all with the same probability)

CriterionExampleWhat it adds
Special dates2026, a 24-12, a wedding dayA number that's easy to remember and to talk about
Birthdays and anniversariesThe birth date of someone you loveAn emotional connection with that person
Numbers with a storyYour front-door number, one you always playThe feeling of 'my usual number'
At randomThe one you're given or the one that catches your eyeZero stress: the excitement, without overthinking

None of these criteria improves the odds: every number, from 00000 to 99999, has exactly the same. Choose the one that excites you most.

Which numbers go first

 

The fact that they all have the same probability doesn't mean they're all always available. Some numbers are in high demand and sell out quickly, especially in a limited edition like the superdécimo. These tend to go first:

→ The current year (for example, 2026): the classic of every campaign.

→ Palindrome numbers and 'round' numbers (such as 12321 or 50000): people like them for how they look.

→ Very special dates on the calendar, which lots of people look for at the same time.

→ Low numbers and popular endings, which get asked for more out of habit.

We'll say it again because it matters: they sell out first because more people ask for them, not because they are luckier. If you like one of these, the only real advantage of buying it early is making sure it doesn't run out.

Choosing with your head: is there a 'luckier' number?

 

No. And this is the most important part of this article. In the Christmas Lottery, every number from 00000 to 99999 has exactly the same probability of winning a prize. There are no 'hot' numbers, no magic endings and no dates that attract luck.

That's why choosing by meaning is perfectly fine —it's part of the excitement—, but it's best not to mistake it for a strategy. Play with your head: spend only what you want to put towards the fun, without chasing 'the good number', because they're all equally good.

How to search for and reserve your number

 

Once you have your criterion, it's time to see what's available. You can search for your Christmas Lottery number to check whether it's free and reserve it. If the one you wanted isn't available, try a variation (turn it around, change a digit) or let yourself be won over by another one you like.

Remember that the Christmas superdécimo is a limited edition: the units are strictly limited. If you're sure about your number and you want it as a superdécimo, the sooner you reserve it, the easier it will be to get.

Frequently asked questions about how to choose your Christmas Lottery number

 
How do I choose the number for my superdécimo?

Whatever excites you most: by a special date, a birthday, a number with a story for you or simply at random. All the criteria are equally valid, because every number has the same probability. The lovely thing about choosing by meaning isn't that it improves your luck, but that it makes the superdécimo more yours.

Are there luckier numbers in the Christmas Lottery?

No. Every number, from 00000 to 99999, has exactly the same probability of winning a prize. There are no 'hot' numbers, magic endings or lucky dates. Choosing by meaning is part of the fun, but it isn't a strategy: there's no way to increase the odds by choosing a particular number.

Which numbers sell out first?

The most sought-after ones: the current year, palindrome numbers and 'round' numbers, very special dates and some low numbers or popular endings. They sell out quickly because lots of people ask for them, not because they bring more luck. With the superdécimo, being a limited edition, it's best not to wait if you want one of these.

Is it better to choose by dates or at random?

For the odds, it makes absolutely no difference: a number chosen by your birthday and one chosen at random have the same chances. So choose by whatever you'll enjoy most. If you fancy the ritual of looking for a date, go ahead; if you'd rather not think about it, random is just as good.

Where can I search for a particular number?

In the office's search tool you can search for your Christmas Lottery number and see whether it's available to reserve. If the one you wanted isn't there, you can try a variation or choose another one you like. Bear in mind that the superdécimo is a limited edition, so availability is whatever it is at any given moment.

What is the superdécimo and how is it different from a décimo?

The superdécimo is an official décimo in a special edition, designed for giving as a gift or collecting. Inside, it's official lottery, with the same probability and the same prize as any décimo; what changes is the presentation. It doesn't bring more luck: it brings more charm.
