The Christmas numbers, in one sentence

There are 100,000 numbers (00000–99999). Several series are printed of each one (in 2026 there are 205), and each series is split into 10 décimos. Result: of a single number there are 2,050 décimos spread across Spain. Figures from the official issue; it's worth confirming them with SELAE.

How many numbers does the Christmas Lottery have?

 

One hundred thousand: from 00000 to 99999. They are all the possible five-figure combinations, starting with 00000. Not one more, not one less: that's why your number always has five digits, even if it starts with zeros.

And although it may seem that there's very little chance of yours coming up, remember what matters: every number has exactly the same probability.

How many décimos are there of each number?

 

In 2026, there are 2,050 décimos of each number. The maths is simple: 205 series × 10 décimos per series = 2,050. That's why there isn't 'one' 13,579: there are 2,050 décimos with that number, spread across Spain, and they all play at the same time.

This explains something very Christmassy: that the same number 'comes up' in several places on the same day. It's not coincidence or magic; it's that the number was in many hands.

Why do the numbers start with zeros?

Because five figures are always used, from 00000 to 99999. This way every number has the same format and confusion is avoided. Your 00025 is just as valid — and has the same probability — as 99999.

Frequently asked questions about the numbers and series of the Christmas Lottery

 
How many numbers does the Christmas Lottery have?

100,000 numbers, from 00000 to 99999. They are all the possible five-figure combinations. Every one has exactly the same probability of winning a prize.

What is a series?

A series is a complete 'copy' of the 100,000 numbers. SELAE prints several each year; in 2026 there are 205. That's why the same number exists in many décimos at once.

How many décimos are there of each number?

In 2026, 2,050 décimos per number: 205 series × 10 décimos per series. They are spread across Spain, and they all play in the same draw.

What's the difference between a full ticket (billete), a décimo and a share?

The full ticket (billete) is the 10 décimos of a number in one series; the décimo is the tenth part (the usual unit, €20); and the share (or fraction) is a part of a décimo, to play for less.

Can the same number win in several cities?

Yes, and it's normal: since there are many décimos of each number (2,050 in 2026), a winning number is usually spread across several towns. It's no coincidence: it was in many hands.

Is one number better for me than another?

For winning, it makes no difference: every number has the same probability. Choose the one you like. That said, the most sought-after ones sell out sooner, so if you want a particular one, it's best to ask for it early.
