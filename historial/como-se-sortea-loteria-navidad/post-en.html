Every 22 December, half of Spain stops to watch the Gordo come out. But how does the Christmas Lottery draw really work? Behind the excitement there is a curious and very carefully prepared procedure, with two drums and thousands of balls.

In this guide we tell you step by step: what is in each drum, how the balls are drawn and called out, in what order the prizes come out and when and where it is held. No technical jargon.

This is an informative explanation of the draw procedure; for the official detail, the reference is always Loterías y Apuestas del Estado (SELAE).

In brief

Two drums, thousands of balls and the San Ildefonso school children

The draw uses two drums: one with 100,000 balls (all the numbers, from 00000 to 99999) and another with 1,807 prize balls. One ball is drawn from each drum at a time, and the boys and girls of the San Ildefonso school call them out. The Gordo can come out at any moment: it is not saved for the end. The draw ends when the prize drum is emptied.

How the balls are drawn and called out

 

This is the best-known part of the draw. From each drum one ball is drawn at a time: one from the number drum and another from the prize drum, at the same time. This is how each number is paired with the prize it wins.

The boys and girls of the San Ildefonso school are the stars: some draw the balls and others sing (call out) them while placing them on the wires set up to display them. The drums only spin again when a wire is complete, and the draw continues, ball by ball, until none are left in the prize drum.

The order of the prizes: why the Gordo can come out at any moment

 

Many people think the Gordo is saved for the end, but that is not the case: it can come out at any moment of the draw. Since each prize ball is paired at random with a number ball, the Gordo appears when its ball comes out of the drum, and that can happen at the start, mid-morning or almost at the close.

The vast majority of the prize balls belong to the pedrea (the many small €1,000 prizes), so the most usual thing is for small prizes to be called out one after another, with the big ones handed out by surprise. If you want to understand how all the prizes are distributed, we cover it in how the Christmas Lottery prizes are distributed.

Who sings (calls out): the boys and girls of San Ildefonso

 

The voices of the draw are those of the pupils of the San Ildefonso school in Madrid, a tradition that goes back a very long way. For years only boys sang; since 1984 girls have taken part too. The way they sing the numbers and the prizes is by now part of the soundtrack of Christmas.

If you have ever wondered where this custom comes from, we tell the story in why the San Ildefonso children sing.

When and where: 22 December at the Teatro Real

 

The Christmas Extraordinary Draw is held on 22 December, in the morning, at the Teatro Real in Madrid. In 2026 it falls on a Tuesday and starts at around 9:00 a.m. It lasts several hours —the draw is long because there are thousands of balls to call out—, so there is plenty of time to follow it calmly and in good company.

How does the Christmas Lottery draw work?

With two drums: one with 100,000 number balls (from 00000 to 99999) and another with 1,807 prize balls. One ball is drawn from each drum at a time, pairing each number with a prize, and the boys and girls of San Ildefonso call them out. The draw ends when the prize drum is emptied.

How many balls are there in the draw's drums?

In the number drum there are 100,000 balls, one for each number from 00000 to 99999. In the prize drum there are 1,807 balls, which include the Gordo, the 2nd, the 3rd, the 2 fourth prizes, the 8 fifth prizes and the 1,794 of the pedrea (prizes of €1,000 per décimo).

In what order do the prizes come out?

At random. Each prize ball is paired with a number ball as they come out, so there is no fixed order. The Gordo can come out at any moment of the draw; it is not held back for the end. The most frequent thing is for pedrea prizes to be called out, with the big ones handed out by surprise.

Who sings the numbers of the Christmas Lottery?

The boys and girls of the San Ildefonso school in Madrid. Some draw the balls from the drums and others call them out while placing them on the wires. It is a tradition of many years; since 1984, girls have taken part too.

At what time and where is the draw held?
