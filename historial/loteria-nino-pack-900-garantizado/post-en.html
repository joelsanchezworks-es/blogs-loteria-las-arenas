What is the Lotería del Niño pack?

 

It's a pack that brings together 100 décimos of the Lotería del Niño — Spain's Epiphany draw — one for every possible ending: from 00 to 99. Instead of choosing one number and crossing your fingers over its ending, you take one of each.

At the official price, a Niño décimo costs €20, so the full pack adds up to €2,000 (100 × €20). The draw takes place on 6 January 2027 and pays its first prize at a rate of €200,000 per décimo. What's interesting isn't the entry price, but what you secure by covering all the endings.

Where the guaranteed €900 comes from

 

The Niño pays out prizes by endings, so, holding one of each, you're bound to land several of them. Here's roughly how that minimum prize breaks down:

✓ 3 refunds (reintegros) → €600. The Niño has three refunds (the last digit of certain prizes and extractions). With 10 décimos for each of those three digits, that's 30 décimos winning €20 each.

✓ 5 two-digit extractions → €200. Since you hold all 100 endings, one of your décimos lands in each of the five two-digit extractions.

✓ The last two digits of the first prize → €100. You'll always hold the décimo whose ending matches the last two digits of the Niño jackpot.

Total: around €900 guaranteed from the outset, almost half of the €2,000. Important: the amounts and the exact number of extractions depend on the official 2027 Niño prize table, so it's worth confirming them with the lottery office. The €900 figure is indicative, not a closed guarantee of outcome.

And all 100 décimos keep playing for the first prize

 

Securing that return doesn't take you out of the big draw. The pack's 100 décimos play for all the Niño's prizes — including the first, worth €200,000 per décimo — just like any other décimo.

Let's be honest: having 100 different endings doesn't multiply your chances at the first prize the way people sometimes think. The top prize falls on one specific number, and you're playing 100 numbers; your odds are those of playing 100 décimos, no more, no less. What the pack secures are the prizes by ending, not the first prize.

Is it a safe investment?

No. It's a lottery, and the lottery isn't an investment. The pack lowers the effective cost and secures part of it (that ~€900), but you're still taking on an outlay that, barring a major prize, you won't get back in full. The big prizes have low odds. It's a limited edition with very few units; play it for what it is, with excitement and responsibility, and only with what you're willing to put toward it.
