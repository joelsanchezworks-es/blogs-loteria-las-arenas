What is a multiple bet and how does it work?

 

A multiple bet is one in which you mark more numbers or stars than the game requires, so that the ticket automatically plays every possible combination between your selections. It is not a trick or a secret method: it is, quite simply, playing many single bets at once with a single ticket.

You will understand it better with La Primitiva. In a single bet you mark 6 numbers. If instead of 6 you mark 7, the system plays every combination of 6 that can be formed from those 7 numbers: that is 7 different combinations, so you are playing 7 bets for €7. In EuroMillions it works the same way, with the twist that you can expand both the numbers (5 to 10) and the stars (2 to 5).

The advantage is obvious: you cover more combinations and your chances of a prize go up. So is the trade-off: the price grows very fast, because you pay for every combination. That is why, before marking extra, it is worth looking at the table and knowing what you are getting into.

Multiple bets in EuroMillions: combinations and price

 

In EuroMillions, the single bet is 5 numbers and 2 stars for €2.50; in a multiple bet you can go up to 10 numbers and 5 stars. The number of combinations shoots up depending on how many numbers and stars you mark, and the price is always those combinations times €2.50. Here is the table with a few common examples:

As you can see, marking just one extra number or one extra star already multiplies the cost. The most common 'entry-level' multiple bet is 6 numbers and 2 stars: six combinations for €15. Beyond that, every extra box weighs heavily on your wallet, so it is a decision worth taking with the table in front of you, not by marking on a hunch.

Multiple bets in La Primitiva: from 7 to 11 numbers

 

In La Primitiva, the single bet is 6 numbers for €1; in a multiple bet you mark 7 to 11 numbers and play every possible combination of 6. Since each combination costs €1, the price matches the number of bets. Here is the full table:

The single bet (6 numbers) is 1 bet for €1. With each number you add, the number of combinations —and the price— grows very quickly: from €7 with 7 numbers to €462 with 11.

The jump is enormous: going from 7 to 11 numbers takes the price from €7 to €462. That is why large multiple bets are almost always played as a group, splitting the cost. And that is exactly why they are the ones most prone to validation and sharing-out mistakes, which is precisely what we are going to look at.

The prize table expands: winning in several categories at once

 

The great strength of the multiple bet is that, by playing many combinations with a single ticket, you can win in several prize categories at once. It is not a case of 'you just pay more': it is that a single winning result is spread across several simultaneous prizes, which expands the prize table compared with a single bet.

An example with La Primitiva makes it clear. Imagine you play a multiple bet of 7 numbers (7 combinations) and you hit it fully: the 6 winning numbers are among your 7. Then one of your combinations has all 6 matches (first category), and the other six have 5 of those numbers plus your seventh, so together they add up to several 5-match prizes at once. With a single ticket you take home one big prize and several medium ones at the same time.

That is the logic of the 'expanded prize': a multiple bet not only increases the probability of winning, but, when you do win, you get paid for several combinations. The cost is higher, yes, but so is the return on a win. The important thing is to understand that this total prize is calculated by adding up what each combination wins separately.

It is worth being honest about what a multiple bet does and does not do. It does expand your coverage: you cover more combinations, so it is more likely that one of them wins, and a win pays off more. What it does not do is change the odds: each specific combination has exactly the same probability as in a single bet, not one bit more. It is not a method for 'forcing' a prize, but a way of playing more combinations at once, paying for all of them. That is why it makes sense when the cost is shared in a group, and that is exactly why you have to keep the accounts straight.

The cost of a multiple bet shoots up very fast. In La Primitiva, 11 numbers is €462 (462 bets), and in EuroMillions the maximum reaches €6,300. Before you validate, and especially in a syndicate, be clear about the exact price and who puts in how much: it is the part where groups fail the most.

Why so many syndicates validate multiple bets wrong

 

The most common mistake is not in marking the numbers, but in how a multiple bet is validated and shared out in a group: because it is a single ticket with many bets and a high price, any slip-up is costly. These are the mistakes we see most often at the office:

A multiple bet is a single ticket, not several bets shared out

The basic misunderstanding is thinking that 'everyone plays their own numbers'. No: a multiple bet is a single ticket that plays all the combinations together, and it generates a single receipt (resguardo). The participants do not have 'their' bet separately, but rather a proportional share of the whole ticket. If this is not understood, sharing out the prize (and the cost) quickly gets tangled, because there are no 'individual bets' to separate.

The single receipt, the cost and the sharing-out

With only one receipt, three classic risks appear. First, safekeeping: if that receipt is lost or kept by someone who cannot be reached, the whole group is left stranded, and a photo is no good for claiming a prize. Second, the cost: an €84 or €462 multiple bet has to be balanced before validating, not 'by eye', because if money is short someone ends up putting in more than their share. And third, the sharing-out: when the multiple bet wins in several categories at once, the total prize has to be shared out according to what each person put in, and without a written agreement that causes arguments at exactly the moment that should be a celebration.

✗Believing each member has 'their own' bet: it is a single ticket; nobody plays loose combinations in their own name.

✗Validating without balancing the exact cost: a €462 multiple bet leaves no room for 'we'll sort it out later'.

✗Leaving the receipt 'in someone's charge' and nothing more: if it is lost or does not turn up in time, everything is lost.

✓Documenting who puts in how much and keeping the receipt safe: with ownership on record, the sharing-out is automatic and hassle-free.

None of these problems has anything to do with luck: they are about organisation. And they are all avoided with the same thing —putting in writing who takes part and in what proportion, and keeping the receipt safe— before validating the multiple bet, not after.

Frequently asked questions about multiple bets

 
How do multiple bets work in EuroMillions?

You mark more than 5 numbers or more than 2 stars and the system plays every possible combination with a single ticket. You can choose from 5 to 10 numbers and 2 to 5 stars, and the price is the number of combinations times €2.50. For example, 6 numbers and 2 stars is 6 combinations (€15); 7 numbers and 3 stars, 63 combinations (€157.50); and the maximum, 10 numbers and 5 stars, is 2,520 combinations (€6,300).

How much does a multiple bet cost in La Primitiva?

In La Primitiva the single bet is 6 numbers for €1. In a multiple bet you mark 7 to 11 numbers and each combination of 6 costs €1: 7 numbers is €7 (7 bets), 8 numbers €28, 9 numbers €84, 10 numbers €210 and 11 numbers €462. The price grows very fast because every extra number multiplies the combinations, so it is worth being clear about the cost before you validate, especially in a syndicate.

Can you win in several categories with a multiple bet?

Yes, and that is its great advantage. Because a multiple bet plays many combinations at once, a single winning result can spread a prize across several categories. For example, in a 7-number multiple bet in La Primitiva, if the 6 winning numbers are among your 7, one combination takes the first category (6 matches) and the other six add up to several 5-match prizes. The total prize is calculated by adding up what each combination wins.

Why do syndicates validate multiple bets wrong?

Because a multiple bet is a single ticket with many bets and a single receipt, not several bets shared out. The typical mistakes are: believing that each member has 'their own' bet, validating without balancing the exact cost (which can run to hundreds of euros) and leaving the receipt in someone's charge without documenting who put in how much. It is all avoided by recording each person's stake and keeping the receipt safe before validating.

Is there a number to win the lottery today in 2026?

There is no such thing. Every combination has the same probability and there are no 'lucky' numbers or formulas for winning. A multiple bet increases the combinations you cover —and the spend— but it guarantees nothing: it is still chance. What you can control is playing in an orderly way and, as a group, validating and sharing out properly. Nobody can sell you the winning number: it is only known after the draw.

How does the 1220 code work to win?

It does not work. The '1220 code' is numerology that circulates on social media and promises to predict results from previous draws. It is false: every draw is pure chance and there is no pattern to 'calculate'. Neither a multiple bet nor any method can guess the winning combination. The only useful thing is to understand how the game works —multiple bets included— and to play with a clear head and clear accounts.
