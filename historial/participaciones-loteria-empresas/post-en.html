Organising Christmas Lottery shares takes more than just dividing the price of a décimo. You need to define the amount staked, identify the décimos that back the issue and establish who coordinates their distribution and any eventual collection.

At Lotería Las Arenas, if your company or organisation wants to take part in this way, we ask you to let us know so we can work out together how to organise it. You shouldn't assume that any campaign of full décimos can simply be turned into a shares campaign. You can start with our company lottery service.

The key point

A share is not another décimo

A share is a fraction of a bet or of a décimo: it lets you play with an amount below the full minimum bet. It is not a new décimo issued by SELAE, nor a simple list of interested people. Before you design anything, define the amount staked, the décimos that back the issue and who coordinates distribution and collection. And, above all, choose the format (full décimos or shares) before preparing the message to your team.

What a share is and what it isn't

 

SELAE defines shares as fractions of a bet or of a décimo. They allow you to take part with an amount below the full minimum bet.

So a share is not another décimo issued by SELAE. Its contents must make clear what part is being staked and in which bet. Nor should it be confused with a simple internal survey, a list of interested people or an email announcing the company's number.

If the company buys full décimos to hand them out individually, it is organising a different arrangement altogether. It's best to choose the format before preparing the design and the message to the team.

Define the amount staked and the scope of the issue

 

Agree how much is staked in each share and how many will be issued. If several numbers are involved, it must be clear how the amount is distributed between them.

As a mathematical example, 100 shares with an amount staked of 5 € represent 500 € of lottery. At 20 € per Christmas décimo, that issue requires backing equivalent to 25 décimos. This is an illustrative scenario, not an offer or a real issue from Las Arenas; the official price of the Christmas décimo appears in SELAE's information.

If you are considering charging an amount different from the amount staked, don't mix the two or present everything paid as lottery. Before launching the campaign, check the applicable setup and requirements. This guide does not assume any surcharges or donations.

Check the backing before distributing

 

The relationship between shares and décimos must be settled before they are distributed. Don't announce shares in a number based solely on an availability request.

As an organisational control, it's advisable to keep the identification of the issue, the décimos that back it and the records needed to track what has been distributed. If booklets are used, it helps to be able to tell them apart and to avoid the same share being recorded twice.

These are control recommendations, not a description of a specific tool included in the service. How to document it should be agreed for your campaign.

What information the participant needs to understand

 

Before buying, the person must know exactly what they are playing. At a minimum, the share must make clear:

→ The draw and year being played.

→ Which number or numbers their share includes.

→ What amount applies to each one (and distinguishing the price paid from the amount staked when they differ).

→ Who the organiser is and how to contact them.

→ The campaign instructions and a clear point of reference for raising questions.

The design must be reviewed before printing or distributing. It's not enough for it to look attractive: a wrong number, a previous year or an ambiguous amount undermine the clarity of the whole campaign.

Physical shares and digital solutions

 

Don't assume that a printed share and one managed through an external platform work the same way. They can differ in access, receipts, distribution and collection procedure.

Our company website should not be confused with third-party services either. When an external platform is used, the communication must identify it and explain the conditions actually agreed.

Don't advertise an app, a digital issue or automatic payment just because that option exists in another service. Ask what can be organised for your case.

Prepare in advance what to do if there's a prize

 

The organiser must be clear about who keeps the décimos, how the shares will be evidenced and what instructions participants will receive if they win. Each share should not be presented as if it were an individual décimo that could be collected interchangeably at any point of sale.

On tax matters, the Spanish Tax Agency (Agencia Tributaria) explains that, in shared prizes, the exempt amount is distributed proportionally among the beneficiaries: a full exemption does not apply to each share of the same décimo.

For specific conditions or tax questions affecting the organising entity, check the matter with your adviser before publishing the collection instructions.

Watch out for the tax treatment of shares. In a shared prize, the exempt portion (the first 40.000 € per décimo) is split in proportion to what each participant staked; it is not applied in full to each share. This is general information: for your entity's specific case, consult an adviser before publishing the collection instructions.
