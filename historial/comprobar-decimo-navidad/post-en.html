How to Check Your Christmas Lottery Ticket Online

 

With an internet connection, checking takes seconds and is almost impossible to get wrong, because the system does the comparison for you. There are two official routes, and both are free and reliable.

Checking online has one more advantage, peace of mind: because the system compares every category for you at once—number, last-digit endings, hundreds group and refund prize—it's almost impossible for a small prize to slip through. That said, always do it through the official channels. In the days after the draw, fake apps and websites imitating the state lottery's own tend to spring up, either to push ads at you or, worse, to ask for your personal or banking details. The official app and website are free and will never ask you for anything odd just to ‘check’ a ticket.

The Official App: Scan the Code

The Spanish State Lottery (Loterías y Apuestas del Estado) app (available for Android and iOS) lets you scan the barcode or the QR code on your ticket. As soon as you point your camera at it, the app instantly tells you whether it has won and how much, checking every category at once. It's the most convenient and secure way to check, because it removes the risk of misreading a digit.

The Official Lottery Website

If you prefer a computer, go to loteriasyapuestas.es, open the check section (‘Comprobar’) and enter your ticket number (and the draw—the Spanish Christmas Lottery). The site will tell you whether it has won and in which category. It's the same official result as the app, just typed in rather than scanned. Trust only this source, not the lists or screenshots doing the rounds on social media: they can contain typos.

How to Check Your Ticket Without Internet

 

Without a connection you can still check your ticket with complete confidence: all you need is the official prize list. On the day of the draw, that list is published in the press and can be consulted in printed form at any lottery office. Checking this way takes a little longer, but it's just as valid if you do it carefully.

The method is simple, though it pays to take your time. First, find your full number in the list of major prizes and in the pedrea (the many small prizes). Then—and this is the step most people forget—check the last-digit endings (the final digits), the hundreds group (the first three digits) and, above all, the refund prize (the final digit). Go digit by digit, without rushing, and if you have any doubt, don't write it off: keep it and bring it to the lottery office.

A tip: when you check by hand, keep the ticket and the list side by side and cross off each category as you go. Most of the ‘I thought I hadn't won’ scares come from checking quickly and by eye, skipping over the very category that held the prize.

And don't feel you have to check on the 22nd itself if you can't. Spanish Christmas Lottery prizes don't expire until three months have passed, so a well-kept ticket can be checked calmly a few days later, with the final list in front of you and none of the rush of the draw morning. A calm, thorough check beats a quick glance amid the nerves.

The Refund Prize and Partial Matches: Don't Write Off Your Ticket

 

In the Spanish Christmas Lottery, it's not only the person who matches the full number who wins: there are many ways to take home a prize on a partial match, and the most common is the refund prize. The refund prize rewards tickets whose final digit matches that of the Gordo: if that's you, you get back the €20 the ticket cost you. It's the smallest prize and the most common, and the one most people throw away without realising.

But it isn't the only one. A ticket that isn't ‘the number’ can win in several ways. This table sums them up:

Ways to win without matching the full number

CategoryHow you win
Refund prize (reintegro)Your final digit matches the Gordo's → you get your €20 back
Last-digit endings (terminaciones)The last two (or three) digits match those of a major prize
Hundreds group (centena)The first three digits match those of a major prize (same hundreds group)
Adjacent numbers (aproximaciones)Your number is immediately before or after the Gordo, 2nd or 3rd prize
PedreaYour full number matches one of the draw's many small prizes

With a single ticket you take part in all these categories at once. That's why, even if you don't have ‘the number’, it's worth checking the whole thing: the last-digit endings, the hundreds group and the refund prize reward a huge number of people every year.

An example makes it crystal clear. Imagine the Gordo is 45,678. If your ticket ends in 8, you have the refund prize and get your €20 back. If it ends in 78, you win on the last-digit endings of the final two digits. If it starts with 456, you're in the hundreds group. And if your number is 45,677 or 45,679, you take an adjacent-number prize. All of that, with the same ticket and without being ‘the winning number’. That's why we keep saying it: a ticket is checked in full, digit by digit, not at a glance.

Don't just look at whether your number is the Gordo. The refund prize (final digit), the last-digit endings and the hundreds group hand prizes to a great many tickets that ‘didn't win’ at first glance. Checking the whole ticket is the difference between claiming a real prize and throwing it away.

The Most Common Checking Mistakes (and How to Avoid Them)

 

Almost every prize that goes unclaimed is lost not in the draw, but at the checking stage. These are the slip-ups we see most often—and the ones you'll now know how to avoid:

✗Looking only at whether it's the Gordo and binning the ticket: you skip the last-digit endings, the hundreds group and the refund prize.

✗Not checking the final digit: that's the refund prize, the most common one, and it gives back what you paid.

✗Trusting an unofficial list or screenshot: these can carry typos or refer to a different draw.

✗Checking in a rush and by eye: misreading a single digit makes people discard winning tickets.

✓Check the full number and the partial matches, using the app, the official website or your lottery office.

The good news is that all these mistakes share the same fix: check with an official source and look at the whole ticket, without leaving out a single category. Five minutes well spent on the day of the draw—or in the days that follow, since there's plenty of time—are worth more than any quick glance. And if you'd rather not risk a slip, you can always leave it to your lottery office: checking tickets is, quite literally, part of our daily work.

What if Your Ticket Has Won? What to Do

 

If, when you check, you find your ticket has won, take a breath and go slowly: there's no rush at all. Spanish Christmas Lottery prizes can be claimed for three months from the day of the draw, so you don't have to dash out on 22 December itself. The first and most important thing is to keep the ticket safe: it's the document that proves your prize, and if it's lost or damaged, claiming becomes much harder.

Where you claim depends on the amount. Prizes of less than €2,000 per ticket are paid directly at lottery offices; those of €2,000 or more go through an authorised bank, where you provide your account details. And bear in mind the tax on large prizes: the portion above €40,000 is taxed at 20%, withheld at the moment of payment. You don't need to worry about the paperwork; the lottery office or the bank guides you through every step.

→ Keep the ticket somewhere safe, and don't sign it or show it around unnecessarily.

→ Be clear on the amount and the category before you go to claim (full number, last-digit endings, hundreds group, refund prize…).

→ Watch out for scams after the draw: no official body will ask you for money up front to ‘process’ your prize.

In short: checking well is half the job; claiming sensibly is the other half. With the ticket kept safe, the category clear and the calm of having three months ahead of you, the prize is yours without any nasty surprises.

Frequently Asked Questions

 
How do you check the Spanish Christmas Lottery officially?

There are three official, reliable routes. The fastest is to scan the code (barcode or QR) on your ticket with the Loterías y Apuestas del Estado app, which checks every category instantly. You can also enter the number on the website loteriasyapuestas.es, in the check section. And without internet, you can compare it against the printed official list or bring it to a lottery office. The key is to trust only the official source, not the lists or screenshots doing the rounds on social media, which can contain typos.

How can I check my Spanish Christmas Lottery number without internet?

With the official prize list, which on the day of the draw is published in the press and can be consulted in printed form at any lottery office. Find your full number in the major prizes and in the pedrea, then check the last-digit endings (final digits), the hundreds group (first three digits) and the refund prize (final digit). Go digit by digit and without rushing; if you have any doubt, don't throw it out: keep it and bring it to the lottery office to check it safely.

What is the refund prize (reintegro) and how do I know if I've won it?

The refund prize rewards tickets whose final digit matches that of the first prize (the Gordo). If that's you, you get your €20 back—the cost of the ticket. It's the smallest prize and also the most common: that's why it's always worth checking the final digit, even when the full number hasn't come up. Many people discard tickets that actually held the refund prize, simply for not looking at that one detail.

Is there a number to win the lottery today in 2026?

There isn't one—not at Christmas either. Tickets come with their number already printed, and they all have the same chance of winning: there are no ‘lucky’ numbers and no tricks for getting it right. What you can control is checking properly the one you have, because that's where prizes are genuinely won (or lost). Anyone selling you ‘today's winning number’ is deceiving you; put that energy into going through your whole ticket, with the official source.

How does the 1220 code work to win?

It doesn't. The ‘1220 code’ (or ‘1220 method’) is numerology that circulates on social media and promises to predict numbers from past results. It's false: every draw is pure chance and the tickets are pre-printed, so there's nothing to ‘calculate’. No method predicts the Gordo. The only thing that's genuinely useful, and free, is checking your ticket with the app, the official website or at your lottery office, without leaving out a single category.
