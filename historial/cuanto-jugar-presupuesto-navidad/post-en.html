How much should you play on the Christmas Lottery? The healthy answer is very simple: whatever you've decided in advance, and not a euro more. Excitement isn't measured in décimos.

The Christmas Lottery is a leisure expense, like going to the cinema or out for dinner: something you fancy and enjoy, not a way to make money. With that idea clear, choosing how much to play is easy.

Here we give you some guidelines to set your budget, understand what each way of playing costs and do it sensibly. Because playing well means, above all, playing responsibly.

Decide your budget before you start

 

The best advice in this whole guide is this: set yourself a limit before you buy. Think about how much you want to spend on the Christmas Lottery this year —as a festive treat— and treat it as a fixed amount.

Three simple rules help: make it money you don't need for other things, don't chase what you've spent by buying more 'just in case', and let the figure be decided by you, not by the emotion of the moment. If you're clear about it in advance, you'll enjoy the draw without any pressure.

Décimo or shares: to suit your budget

 

If you want to keep spending down, shares (participaciones) are your ally. They're part of a décimo, so you can play the same number for a smaller amount, on their own or by combining several.

The full décimo (€20) is the classic option and gives you the whole unit. Neither is better than the other: it depends on how much you've decided to play. What matters is that your budget drives the choice, and not the other way round. To understand the odds behind it, we explain them in the chances of winning the Christmas Lottery.

Playing in a group: more excitement, less spending

 

Sharing a décimo with family, friends or colleagues is one of the best ways to play sensibly. You split the cost between several people, so each one puts in less, and the excitement multiplies.

That said, to avoid any mix-ups: put in writing who's taking part and with how much, and keep a photo or copy of the décimo. Playing in a group lowers individual spending and, along the way, turns the draw into a shared occasion.

Responsible gambling: it's leisure, not an investment

 

It's worth saying plainly: the Christmas Lottery is not a way to make money, and the odds of the big prizes are low. Playing is fine as part of the excitement of the festive season, always within a limit you can afford.

Playing responsibly means exactly that: treating it as a leisure expense, not expecting to win, not playing to 'win it back' and keeping it to an amount that doesn't affect your day-to-day life. It's a game only for those over 18. If at any point you feel it's getting out of hand, you'll find information and help at juegoseguro.es.

Frequently asked questions about how much to play on the Christmas Lottery

 
How much does it cost to play on the Christmas Lottery?

A décimo costs €20, which is the most common unit. If you want to play with less, shares (participaciones) let you join in for a smaller amount (part of a décimo). The full ticket —the 10 décimos of the same number— costs €200. You choose according to your budget.

How do I set a budget for the lottery?

Decide before you buy how much you want to set aside, treat it as a fixed amount and make it money you don't need for other things. Don't chase what you've spent by buying more, and let the figure be set by you, not by the excitement of the draw. With a clear limit, you'll enjoy it much more.

Is it better to buy a décimo or shares?

It depends on how much you want to play. Shares (participaciones) are ideal for spending less (they're part of a décimo); the full décimo (€20) is the classic option. Neither is better than the other: what matters is that your budget drives the choice.

Does playing more increase my odds of winning?

Buying more entries slightly increases your chances, but also your spending, and the odds of the big prizes remain low. The Christmas Lottery is not a way to make money: the sensible thing is to play for fun, within a limit you can afford.

What is responsible gambling?

It's playing while treating it as leisure, not as an investment: with a limit decided in advance, with money you don't need, without expecting to win and without playing to 'win it back'. It's a game only for those over 18. If you notice it's no longer fun, you'll find help at juegoseguro.es.
