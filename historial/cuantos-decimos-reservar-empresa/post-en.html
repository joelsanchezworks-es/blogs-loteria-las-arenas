There is no right number of lottery tickets (décimos) for every company, nor any reliable 'one per employee' rule. To prepare the reservation, use the actual purchases from previous years and check them against your team's interest. Then ask about availability for that quantity.

At Lotería Las Arenas you can begin your request with an indicative figure. The important thing is to distinguish it from a confirmed reservation and from the purchases participants actually end up making. You can start with our company lottery service.

The key

Start from what was bought, not from what was reserved

The best reference is what was genuinely bought in previous campaigns, not what was reserved. If there is no track record, gauge your team's interest without committing anyone to buy. An indicative figure lets you begin the request, but it is not a confirmed reservation and does not oblige anyone to pay. The aim is not to reserve as much as possible, but a quantity that is proportionate to demand and to the agreement with the lottery office.

Start from the tickets sold, not those reserved

 

If 200 tickets were reserved last year and 140 were bought, it is unwise to treat 200 as real demand. The useful reference is what was actually bought, together with the circumstances of that campaign.

Ask what has changed: team size, participating sites, the purchase method or whether there is a company gift. A change in staff numbers alone does not let you calculate the rise in demand.

Set aside any one-off orders too. A single purchase for an event or a client need not be repeated this year.

If there is no track record, gauge interest without committing anyone to buy

 

You can ask who would be interested and how many tickets they would like to buy. Make it clear that their answer helps prepare a forecast — not that a ticket is already reserved in their name, nor that it obliges them to pay.

A simple survey provides more information than assuming a universal participation rate. There is no need to collect personal documentation to estimate your staff's interest.

Keep participation voluntary and avoid inflating the forecast with messages that encourage people to spend beyond their own budget.

The forecast is 85 tickets, not 60 (because some people want more than one). At 20 € per ticket, those 85 tickets would represent 1.700 € of lottery if they were all bought. The calculation does not include any costs associated with delivery, nor does it turn the forecast into an order; you can buy Christmas Lottery online and check the official price, which is set by SELAE.

Add the order the company will pay for separately

 

If the company wishes to give tickets as a gift, budget that part independently. Otherwise, you may count the same recipient twice, or present as a voluntary purchase what was actually a gift.

For example, 30 tickets for a corporate gift are a separate order from the estimate of 85 that employees would buy. They should only be added together if they really are additional purchases and the budget has been approved.

Do not add a buffer without agreeing its terms

 

You may want to reserve a buffer for new joiners or undecided people, but do not apply it as an automatic formula or announce unlimited top-ups.

Before increasing the quantity, ask how that buffer will be handled, how long it would stay reserved and what terms would apply to anything not bought. A reservation does not by default carry a right to a refund.

The availability of the number can also limit the volume. It is better to resolve this before handing out the poster to employees.

Use what you learn for the next campaign

 

When the campaign ends, keep a record of the quantity finally bought and any issues that affected participation. That way you will be able to distinguish a forecast that ran too high from a specific problem with communication or access.

The aim is not to reserve as much as possible. It is to arrange a quantity that is proportionate to demand and to the agreement with the lottery office.

Frequently asked questions about how many tickets to reserve

 
Does the 'one ticket per employee' rule work?

It is not reliable. There is no right number for every company, nor any universal 'one per employee' rule: some people do not take part and some buy several tickets. What works best is to start from the actual purchases of other years and, if there is no track record, to gauge interest across the team without committing anyone to buy.

Should I start from the tickets reserved or those sold last year?

From those sold. If 200 tickets were reserved last year but 140 were bought, the real reference is 140, not 200. It is also worth looking at what has changed (team size, sites, purchase method, company gift) and setting aside the one-off orders, such as a single purchase for an event, which need not be repeated.

How do I work out the forecast if I only know how many people are interested?

Count the expected tickets, not just the people. In our example, 40 people want one ticket, 15 want two and 5 want three: that is 60 people but 85 tickets. At 20 € per ticket, those 85 tickets would represent 1.700 € of lottery if they were all bought. It is an indicative estimate, not an order.

Is the company gift added to the employees' forecast?

Only if it is an additional order with an approved budget. It is best to budget the company gift separately: for example, 30 tickets for a corporate gift are a separate order from the 85 that employees would buy. If they are not kept apart, you may count the same recipient twice, or present as a voluntary purchase what was in fact a gift.

Is it worth reserving a buffer just in case?

It can make sense for new joiners or undecided people, but not as an automatic formula or by announcing unlimited top-ups. Before increasing the quantity, agree how that buffer will be handled, how long it would stay reserved and what happens to anything not bought: a reservation does not by default carry a right to a refund. In addition, the availability of the number can limit the volume.

Is an indicative figure already a reservation?

No. An indicative figure is used to begin the request, but it must be distinguished from a confirmed reservation and from the purchases participants finally make. At Las Arenas we check availability for that quantity before confirming the corresponding reservation.
