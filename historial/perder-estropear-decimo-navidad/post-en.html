If your ticket is damaged (wet, torn, code rubbed off)

 

A damaged ticket doesn't mean you lose the prize: if the ticket can be identified, it pays out. Here are the steps:

1

Don't tamper with it

Don't try to glue it, piece it back together, clean it or dry it with heat. You could damage it further and make it harder to authenticate.

2

Protect it

Keep it as it is, inside a paper or plastic envelope, so it doesn't get worse on the way to the lottery office.

3

Present it at a point of sale

Go to a lottery office with the damaged ticket or receipt and fill in the "Solicitud de pago de premios".

4

Wait for authentication

Loterías checks that it's genuine. If an identifiable part remains, it can validate it; once verified, it pays out just like one in perfect condition.

What if only the QR or barcode rubs off? The ticket is also identified by its number and series, so if those details are legible, all is not lost. Even so, to cash it you need the physical ticket, authenticated.

If you lose the ticket

 

Here the news is less good: the ticket is a bearer document. That means the prize belongs to whoever presents the ticket, not necessarily to whoever bought it. Without the physical ticket, you can't claim the prize: presenting it is essential.

That's why, if you lose it, your options are very limited. There's no "duplicate" that gives you back the right to collect. Prevention is almost everything: always knowing which number you're playing and where your ticket is.

If it's stolen (and the deadline you mustn't forget)

 

If your ticket is stolen, file a police report with the authorities as soon as possible. It's the way to try to protect your right, although, since it's a bearer instrument, recovery is complicated.

And a reminder that catches someone out every year: the right to collect Christmas lottery prizes expires three months on, counted from the day after the draw. A winning ticket forgotten in a drawer stops being worth anything once that deadline passes.
