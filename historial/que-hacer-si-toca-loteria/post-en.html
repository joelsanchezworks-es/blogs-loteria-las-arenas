The first 7 steps if you win the lottery

 

In the excitement, it's easy to skip the basics. This is the sensible sequence, step by step:

1

Breathe and don't post about it

Avoid sharing it on social media or open groups. Discretion is your best protection in the first few hours.

2

Put your décimo somewhere safe

Keep it in a safe place and take a photo of both sides. The décimo is a bearer instrument (al portador): whoever holds it, collects.

3

Check the prize through an official source

Use an official checker or the Loterías app. To check, all you need is the number; never your bank details.

4

If it's shared, put it in writing

If several of you were playing, write down who's in and for how much before claiming. Avoid misunderstandings at the very best moment.

5

Don't make big decisions all at once

No impulse purchases or promises made in the heat of the moment. The money isn't going anywhere in a few days.

6

Learn about deadlines and taxes

There's a deadline to claim and a portion that's taxed above a certain amount. You'll find it further down, with links for the details.

7

Seek advice if the prize is large

For large amounts, consult a professional (and your bank for claiming). Good advice at the right time saves trouble.

Deadlines and taxes: get informed (without the stress)

 

Two things are worth looking at calmly: how long you have to claim and what portion is taxed. We won't go into detail here so as not to overwhelm you —this post is about first steps—, but we've left you the guides with the details.

Check the deadline to claim a lottery prize and, for the tax side, the taxes on the Christmas Lottery. This is general information; for your specific case, it's better to get advice.

Where and when do you claim?

 

Smaller prizes are claimed at the lottery office; large amounts, through a bank, which will ask you to identify yourself. Keep your décimo in good condition: it's the document that proves the prize.

There's no rush on the day itself: you have a deadline to claim (you'll find it in the guide above). The urgent thing isn't to hurry, it's to do it right.

The most common mistake in the first 24 hours

If we had to point to a single mistake, it would be this: telling people too soon and making decisions in the heat of the moment. Posting about it, promising money, signing things… all of that can wait. Keep your décimo safe, check it carefully, and give yourself a few days to think with a clear head. The excitement won't expire in 24 hours.

Frequently asked questions about what to do if you win the lottery

 
What's the first thing I should do if I win the lottery?

Keep your décimo safe and don't post about it. Put it somewhere secure, take a photo of it, and check the prize through an official source. With that done, everything else (deadlines, taxes, claiming) can wait until you look at it calmly.

Should I tell people I've won?

The less, the better, especially in the first few hours. Discretion protects you. Avoid social media and open groups; there'll be time to celebrate with your loved ones.

Where do I claim the prize?

Smaller prizes are claimed at the lottery office; large amounts, through a bank, which asks you to identify yourself. Keep your décimo safe: it's what proves the prize.

Do I have to pay taxes?

Lottery prizes are taxed above a certain amount; below that, they're exempt. You'll find the details in our guide to the taxes on the Christmas Lottery. This is general information; for your case, consult a professional.

How long do I have to claim?

There's a deadline to claim the prize (we explain it in the linked guide). You don't need to rush on the day itself, but don't leave it until the last moment either. Better to do it with time to spare.

What if the décimo was shared?

Put in writing who's in and for how much before claiming, and consider claiming it through the bank so it distributes the money among the owners. That way the split isn't interpreted as a gift.
